The **Women's Scottish Open** stopped being a shared little early-week puzzle and turned into a **Jenny Shin** tournament in a hurry.

According to **LPGA reporting published Friday, July 24, 2026**, plus an **Associated Press recap from Friday** and the **official LPGA leaderboard** checked on **Saturday, July 25, 2026**, Shin followed her opening **66** with a **5-under 67** to reach **11-under par** and take a **five-shot lead through 36 holes** at **Dundonald Links**.

That is a halfway lead with some teeth.

This piece is based on the **LPGA's July 24, 2026 halfway-lead coverage**, the **AP's Friday recap**, and the **official LPGA leaderboard**, all checked on **Saturday, July 25, 2026**. No pretending I was walking Scottish fairways in three layers counting bounce-outs by hand.

## Shin Turned a Shared Lead Into a Gap

The useful part of Friday is not just that Shin stayed in front.

It is how cleanly she separated.

LPGA reporting says Shin opened the week with a **6-under 66** and then added a **67** in the second round. The AP recap says Friday's card started with a **bogey**, then settled down into **six birdies**, including one on her final hole, to get her to **11-under**.

That is the kind of round that tells the rest of the field the door is no longer standing open by default.

One bad hole early.
No panic.
Back to making birdies.
Back to owning the board.

## The Chasers Are Real, but They Are Also Five Back

The official **LPGA leaderboard**, last updated at **6:33 a.m. UTC on Saturday, July 25**, had:

- **Jenny Shin** at **-11**
- **Esther Henseleit** at **-6**
- **A Lim Kim** at **-5**
- **Pajaree Anannarukarn** at **-4**
- **Patty Tavatanakit** at **-4**
- **Erika Hara** at **-4**
- **Lauren Coughlin** at **-4**

That is not a dead leaderboard.

It is just one that now asks somebody else to do something more dramatic than simply keep pace.

The AP recap says **Henseleit** shot **68** with **two birdies and an eagle** to sit second. **A Lim Kim** stayed third at **5-under** after an **even-par round**. And **Coughlin**, who had shared the first-round lead with Shin, dropped back to **4-under** after shooting **2-over** Friday.

So there are names there.
There is quality there.
There is just also a very obvious person in control right now.

## This Is a Better Weekend Shape Than Another Bunched 36-Hole Board

Not every golf tournament gets better when the lead is wide.

This one might.

Dundonald looked firm and mean on Thursday, and the scoring split by Friday makes the tournament feel more defined rather than less interesting. The Scottish Open does not need fake volatility to justify itself. It needs a real course, a legit field, and at least one player taking the conditions seriously enough to force the rest of the event to react.

That player is Shin.

And a five-shot gap at halfway does something useful for the weekend:

- it puts pressure on the chasers immediately
- it gives every Shin bogey actual weight
- and it lets the tournament ask whether somebody can really go catch her instead of merely surviving in a crowd

That is a cleaner competitive shape than a six-way tie at **7-under** where nothing feels earned yet.

## The Big Names Did Not All Cooperate

This is part of why the halfway board feels sharper.

The AP recap says **Nelly Korda** finished with **four straight bogeys** in a **76** and sat at **4-over**, which was **two above the cut line** at the time. The same recap said defending champion **Lottie Woad** was tied for **11th at even par** after a **2-under 70**.

That does not kill the tournament.

It just makes the current story less dependent on the usual top-of-poster names and more dependent on the woman who actually played the place best for two days.

That is allowed.
It is also usually healthier.

## Shin Has Made the "Warm-Up Week" Framing Look Dumber

We already argued in [our Tuesday opinion piece](/blog/womens-scottish-open-not-a-warm-up-opinion-2026/) that the **Women's Scottish Open** should not be presented like a polite appetizer before next week's **AIG Women's Open**.

Halfway boards like this are why.

If one player reaches **11-under**, opens a five-shot lead, and starts forcing a real chase on a legitimate Scottish links setup, then the event is doing exactly what a serious standalone tournament is supposed to do. It is building its own tension instead of waiting for the next venue to validate it.

That is a much better use of the week.

## Bottom Line

**Jenny Shin** followed her opening **66** with a **67** on **Friday, July 24, 2026**, according to **LPGA reporting**, **AP's recap**, and the **official leaderboard** checked **Saturday, July 25**, reaching **11-under** and taking a **five-shot lead through 36 holes** at the **Women's Scottish Open**.

The board behind her still has enough quality to make the weekend interesting.

But at halfway, this tournament belongs to Shin first and everybody else second.
