There is a specific kind of golf-trip mistake that Pinehurst No. 10 is waiting to punish.

You show up because the place looks cool as hell, you tell yourself "I came here to take it on," and then you spend four hours making proud little decisions that are just aggressive enough to become expensive.

That is not the right way to meet **Pinehurst No. 10**.

This is not a fake firsthand guide where I pretend I just walked off the Sandmines with secret access to every contour and every caddie-yardage whisper. This is a practical first-timer guide built from Pinehurst's current official **No. 10** course page, **FAQ**, **Buddy Trip itinerary**, and current **package** pages checked on **Wednesday, August 5, 2026**.

The question is simple:

**If you are playing Pinehurst No. 10 for the first time, what do you actually need to know before the course starts collecting doubles from your ego?**

## Quick Answer

For most first-timers, the smart No. 10 plan is:

- book it as part of a proper **resort stay**, not a fantasy loophole
- assume you are **walking the whole day**
- seriously consider a **caddie or forecaddie**
- start from a tee that leaves you in the **6,100- to 6,400-yard** neighborhood unless you are a real carry-it player
- and play the course with a **below-the-hole, no-short-side, no-hero** approach

## The First Thing to Know: This Is Still a Resort-Guest Round

Pinehurst's current FAQ says that to play **Courses No. 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, or 10**, you must be a **member or resort guest**.

So no, No. 10 is not the sneaky public back door into the Pinehurst prestige lane.

That matters because trip shape comes first.

Pinehurst's current **Premier Golf Package** says it includes **all advance and replay rounds** and bundles **all golf fees and course premiums**, specifically calling out the chance to **discover the new No. 10**. The current **Donald Ross Golf Package** says a **$125 premium applies to rounds on No. 10** and that a **minimum stay of 2 nights is required to play No. 2, No. 4, and No. 10**.

That gives you the practical answer:

- **Premier** makes the most sense if No. 10 is part of a high-volume golf trip
- **Donald Ross** still works if you want a more classic three-round build and do not mind the No. 10 premium
- either way, this is a real trip-planning round, not a casual drop-in

## The Second Thing: Walking Is Not a Side Detail

Pinehurst's current FAQ says **No. 10 is a walking only course**.

The resort's current **Buddy Trip itinerary** goes even more direct: **"As a walking-only course, we recommend booking a caddy in advance."**

That is not marketing fluff. That is Pinehurst politely telling you what kind of day this is.

The current FAQ also lists:

- **caddie: $85 per bag**
- **forecaddie: $45 per player**
- **single bag: $100**

My first-timer take:

- take a **caddie** if this is a one-shot Pinehurst trip and you want the cleanest experience
- take a **forecaddie** if the group wants help without fully lighting money on fire
- carry your own bag only if walking golf is actually normal for you and not something you suddenly become "into" on destination trips

## The Tee-Choice Mistake Most First-Timers Make

Pinehurst's current No. 10 course page lists these headline yardages:

- **Blue: 7,020 yards**
- **Blue/White: 6,709 yards**
- **White: 6,439 yards**
- **White/Green: 6,111 yards**

The dumb first-timer move is picking the longest tee that still feels emotionally flattering.

The smart move is picking the tee that lets you hit approaches with something better than survival-club golf on a course you have never seen before.

My practical starting rule:

- if your normal drive is more like **225 to 240** than **250-plus carry**, **White** is the adult answer
- if you are a stronger player with real carry and still want the round to feel playable, **Blue/White** is the sharper first-visit test
- if you need the **Blue** tee to feel validated, be honest about whether you are buying challenge or just buying extra 4-iron approaches into a place you do not know yet

That is an inference from the current tee-yardage jump, not a direct Pinehurst instruction. But it is the kind of inference that saves strokes.

## The No-Hero Miss Is the Whole Day

Here is the first-timer rule I would write on the yardage book:

**On No. 10, your bad miss cannot be high, short-sided, and stubborn at the same time.**

This is not the place to keep telling yourself that a tucked pin is "there if I just pull it off."

Your stock plan should be:

- favor the part of the green that leaves the ball **below the hole**
- miss **center-side**, not short-side
- take the longer putt over the shaved-collection-area recovery speech

This is the same adult golf behind [how to play firm greens without short-siding yourself](/blog/how-to-play-firm-greens-without-short-siding-yourself/), [how to play front pins without making bogey](/blog/how-to-play-front-pins-without-making-bogey/), and [how to play your first three holes without starting stupid](/blog/how-to-play-your-first-three-holes-without-starting-stupid/).

No. 10 is not where you prove bravery.  
It is where you prove you can still think while the property is trying to distract you.

## My First-Timer Script for the Round

### 1. Through six holes, play for zero doubles

That is the whole opening goal.

Not "I need to make birdie while I still have energy."
Not "I came here to take driver everywhere."

Just:

- keep the card moving
- respect the walk
- let the course show you how far the ball is really releasing that day

If you are **+3 or better through 6 with no doubles**, you are doing fine.

### 2. On par 5s, lay up to a number you actually own

Unless the lie is clean, the angle is obvious, and the shot truly fits, do not turn No. 10 par 5s into fantasy-hero auditions.

I would rather leave:

- **95 to 110 yards**
- or **110 to 125 yards**

...than hit one brave idiot shot and another recovery.

If you need that decision tree refreshed before the trip, read [when to go for par 5s in two](/blog/when-to-go-for-par-5s-in-two/).

### 3. Treat the longer carries like course architecture, not insults

No. 10 is allowed to ask hard questions.

You do not answer them by swinging harder than your pattern.
You answer them by:

- choosing the right tee
- using the right club
- and accepting the hole the course is actually offering

There is a huge difference between "difficult" and "I made this harder because I picked the wrong battleground."

## The One Practice Block I Would Do Before the Trip

If you only get one prep session before Pinehurst No. 10, make it this:

### The 12-ball scoring-club ladder

Hit:

- **4 balls to 95 yards**
- **4 balls to 110 yards**
- **4 balls to 125 yards**

Rules:

- every ball has to finish inside a **30-foot circle**
- if more than **3 of 12** finish outside that window, do it again

Why this drill?

Because destination golf gets expensive when you keep turning sensible layups into nervy half-wedge guesses.
