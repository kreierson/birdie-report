# TaylorMade Qi Irons Review

TaylorMade does not sell a Qi35 iron. The club this page covers is the **Qi iron**. [TaylorMade's Qi irons page](https://www.taylormadegolf.com/Qi-Irons/N2798409.html?lang=en_US) uses that name. **Qi35** is the 2025 driver, fairway, and hybrid family, which TaylorMade introduced in its [Qi35 fairway woods and hybrids announcement](https://www.taylormadegolf.com/clubhouse/890709-taylormade-introduces-qi35-fairway-woods-and-hybrids.html?lang=en_US).

This URL still says `taylormade-qi35-irons-review`. The slug is unchanged so existing links keep working. The product name on the page is Qi irons.

This is a **research-based** review of TaylorMade's published Qi irons materials. It is not a launch-monitor test, a robot test, or a record of rounds played. Earlier copy on this page described eight rounds, Sand Valley dispersion, carry yardages, and a 28.5° 7-iron. Those claims are removed. The repo does not have measurement data for them, and 28.5° does not match TaylorMade's loft table.

## Which TaylorMade "Qi" club is which

TaylorMade's current club list separates these models. Mixing them up is how a woods head ends up in an irons cart.

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Qi irons** | Game-improvement irons. Cap Back Design, Speed Pocket, FLTD CG. |
| **Qi HL irons** | A separate, lighter, higher-launch iron with weaker lofts. The 7-iron is 30.0°. |
| **Qi Max and Qi Max HL** | 2026 irons on TaylorMade's spec sheets. Not the same loft table as the Qi irons. |
| **Qi35 driver, fairway, rescue** | 2025 woods and hybrids. Not irons. |
| **Qi35 Combo Set** | Qi35 hybrids paired with Qi irons. The iron heads in that combo are still Qi irons. |
| **Qi10** | The previous woods family. Not an iron. |

TaylorMade's [Qi HL irons page](https://www.taylormadegolf.com/Qi-HL-Irons/V9897107.html?lang=en_US) describes an ultralight package and increased lofts for moderate swing speeds. The [2026 Qi Max irons spec sheet](https://www.taylormadegolf.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-TMaG-Library/en_US/v1789877478245/docs/productspecs/2026/irons/2026-Qi-Max-Irons.pdf) lists a 28° 7-iron and a 42.5° pitching wedge, with an 8-iron at 32.5°. That is a different set from the Qi irons table below. The model-year index is on [TaylorMade's product specs page](https://www.taylormadegolf.com/product-specs.html?lang=en_US).

## What TaylorMade says the Qi irons are

On the Qi irons product pages, including SKUs such as [N2798309](https://www.taylormadegolf.com/Qi-Irons/N2798309.html?lang=en_US) and [V9849007](https://www.taylormadegolf.com/Qi-Irons/V9849007.html?lang=en_US), TaylorMade describes the set as game-improvement irons aimed at straight distance. The published construction is:

- **Integrated design.** Organic face geometries, Cap Back Design, and a Speed Pocket, tuned head by head for gapping and ball speed.
- **Cap Back Design.** Hollow-body and cavity-back construction used with **HYBRAR Echo Dampers** for sound and feel.
- **FLTD CG.** Center of gravity lowest in the long irons and progressively higher through the scoring clubs.

TaylorMade's on-page comparison chart places Qi irons in the game-improvement column, with high forgiveness, high trajectory, low workability, and the longest distance label inside that TaylorMade chart. Those are TaylorMade's category labels, not a measured ranking against Ping, Callaway, or Mizuno.

The same chart rates **Qi HL** as the highest forgiveness and highest trajectory option in that TaylorMade group, still with low workability. Golfers who want more workability are pointed at other TaylorMade irons on that chart, including the P·790 (players distance) and P·770 (modern players). There is no Qi35 Tour iron. Qi35 Tour is a fairway wood.

## Lofts and stock build

These lofts are the men's Qi irons table repeated across TaylorMade's Qi irons SKUs. They are not launch-monitor carries.

| Club | Loft | Lie | Men's length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 18.5° | 61.5° | 39.125" |
| 5 | 21.0° | 62.0° | 38.50" |
| 6 | 24.0° | 62.5° | 37.88" |
| 7 | 28.0° | 63.0° | 37.25" |
| 8 | 32.0° | 63.5° | 36.75" |
| 9 | 37.0° | 64.0° | 36.25" |
| PW | 43.0° | 64.5° | 35.75" |
| AW | 49.0° | 64.5° | 35.50" |
| SW | 54.0° | 64.5° | 35.25" |
| LW | 59.0° | 64.5° | 35.00" |

TaylorMade also lists women's lengths one inch shorter, and stock swing weights that change by shaft material. Confirm the build sheet for the SKU you order.

A 28.0° 7-iron and a 43.0° pitching wedge are strong relative to older iron sets. The gap from the pitching wedge to a separate gap wedge is a fitting question, not something this page measured on a course. Budget a wedge check before you assume a 50° wedge still slots in.

Published stock shafts on the Qi irons page:

- **KBS Max 85 MT**, stiff and regular, listed at 93g, high launch and high spin
- **Fujikura Ventus TR Blue HB**, stiff 76g, regular 67g, senior 57g, mid launch and mid spin
- **Fujikura Speeder NX TCS 40**, ladies, 49g

Published stock grips include the Lamkin Crossline 360 (47g) and Lamkin ST Soft (40g).

## Who the published design fits

Use TaylorMade's own categories, then confirm them in a fitting.

The Qi iron is the set to compare if you want TaylorMade's game-improvement iron: high trajectory, high forgiveness on TaylorMade's chart, and low workability. The FLTD CG story is specifically about easier long-iron launch and more control in the scoring clubs.

Look at **Qi HL** instead if the goal is a lighter build and weaker lofts for moderate swing speed. That is a different head. The Qi HL 7-iron is 30.0° and the pitching wedge is 44.5°, and the published set runs 5-iron through sand wedge.

Look at **P·790** or **P·770** if the chart's low workability rating is a problem. Look at the **2026 Qi Max** only after you compare its loft table with the Qi table above. A 42.5° Qi Max pitching wedge is not the 43.0° Qi pitching wedge.

If the shortlist also includes other current game-improvement irons, continue with [Mizuno JPX925 Hot Metal vs TaylorMade Qi irons](/blog/mizuno-jpx925-hot-metal-vs-taylormade-qi35-irons/) and [Callaway Elyte vs TaylorMade Qi irons](/blog/callaway-elyte-vs-ping-g440-irons/#callaway-elyte-vs-taylormade-qi-irons).

## Verdict

The Qi irons are TaylorMade's game-improvement iron, with published Cap Back construction, a 28.0° 7-iron, and a 43.0° pitching wedge. Qi35 is a woods and hybrids name. Qi HL and Qi Max are other irons.

**Rating: 9.1/10** as an editorial research score for that published game-improvement design. It is not a score from a fitting session or a tracked set of rounds.
