The **WeatherSof** wins for most golfers because the gap between **$9.95** and **$25** is large in a category where you are absolutely going to buy more than one glove over time.

FootJoy's current WeatherSof sale page still gives you a practical story:

- **advanced performance leather** where the glove takes abuse
- **PowerNet mesh** for breathability
- **FiberSof** material for fit and softness
- **ComforTab** closure

That is a lot of grown-up glove language for a cheap buy.

So if your actual question is:

- which glove is smarter for casual rounds
- which glove is easier to rotate and replace
- which glove gives you the better cost-to-function deal

then the answer is **WeatherSof**.

## Where the Perma-Soft Earns the Higher Spend

The **Perma-Soft** is the smarter answer once you decide you want a nicer glove, not merely a cheaper one.

Titleist's current product page leans on:

- **durable cabretta leather**
- **CoolMax mesh** across the knuckles
- dual rows of elastic
- satin reinforcement in key wear areas

That is a better everyday-leather story than the WeatherSof is trying to tell.

The **Perma-Soft** is not really battling to be the cheapest.

It is battling to be the most sensible step-up glove for golfers who want:

- real leather feel
- decent breathability
- better finish quality

## Feel: Perma-Soft Wins, But It Should

This part is not complicated.

The **Perma-Soft** is the nicer-feeling glove.

This is where the gap tightens.

So the comfort split looks like this:
