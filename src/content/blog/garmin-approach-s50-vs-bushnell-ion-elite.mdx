## Quick Verdict

For most golfers, I would buy the **Bushnell iON Elite** first.

If you specifically want one watch that covers golf **and** normal-life wearable duty, then the **Garmin Approach S50** is the better purchase.

That is the whole page.

The **iON Elite** is the smarter golf-only value at its current **$219.99** official price because it already gives you:

- slope-compensated yardages
- HoleView with Shot Planning
- a color touchscreen
- **38,000+** preloaded courses
- **12+ hours** of battery

The **S50** is the better watch, but it costs **$399.99**, and Garmin's own current page pushes its richer **PlaysLike Distance**, enhanced CourseView maps, and touch targeting through **Garmin Golf membership**.

So the cleaner buying question is not "which is better?" It is "do you want a golf watch, or do you want a golf smartwatch?"

## Why the iON Elite Wins for Most Golfers

Because most golfers do not need their golf watch to become a second lifestyle platform.

Bushnell's current official page makes the iON Elite case very simple:

- **Slope compensated distances**
- **HoleView** with **Shot Planning**
- **38,000+** preloaded courses
- **12+ hrs** battery
- color touchscreen
- **$219.99**

That is a strong golf-only package.

More importantly, it is a package that answers the question most golfers are actually trying to solve:

"How do I get useful numbers on the course without pulling out my phone or spending six hundred bucks?"

That is why the iON Elite remains the saner recommendation for a lot of weekend golfers, walkers, and casual league players.

## Why the S50 Still Has a Very Real Lane

The **S50** is not just a prettier Garmin.

Garmin's current S50 page still gives you:

- a **1.2-inch AMOLED** display
- **43,000+** preloaded courses
- distances to front, middle, and back
- hazard and layup context
- optional **CT10** or **CT1** tracking compatibility
- wrist-based **heart rate**
- **stress**, **Body Battery**, and sleep features
- **Morning Report**
- activity profiles beyond golf
- up to **15 hours** of GPS battery

That is a much broader ownership story.

So if you want one watch that handles golf, training, and everyday life without feeling like a dedicated golf gadget, the **S50** starts making more sense fast.

## The Membership Caveat Matters More Than Garmin Would Prefer

This is the part worth saying clearly.

Garmin's current page markets the S50 with **PlaysLike distance**, but its own feature detail also ties **PlaysLike Distance**, **touch targeting**, and enhanced full-color **CourseView** maps to **Garmin Golf membership**.

That does not kill the S50.

But it does weaken the lazy "just buy Garmin because more features" argument.

Because the iON Elite gives you a very obvious golf benefit right away: **slope yardages out of the box**.

So if the thing pushing you toward the S50 is purely golf performance, you need to be honest about whether you actually want Garmin's broader ecosystem or whether you are just getting hypnotized by the better screen.

## This Is Really Golf Value Versus Better Wearable Value

That is the cleanest way to think about it.

### Buy the iON Elite if you want:

- the cheaper golf-first watch
- slope-compensated yardages without extra nonsense
- a dedicated golf tool
- less money tied up in wrist tech

### Buy the S50 if you want:

- the better everyday watch
- a richer Garmin ecosystem
- wellness and activity tracking
- one device that matters away from the course too

That is why both can be good purchases without being the right purchase for the same golfer.
