they currently cost basically the same money.

L.A.B.'s stock **DF3** page is sitting at **$399**.

Odyssey's direct **Rossie V T DB** page is sitting at **$399.99 on sale** from **$449.99**.

That makes this less of a price fight and more of a help-style decision.

This comparison is **research-based** and built from the current official L.A.B. **DF3 stock** page, L.A.B. **DF3 custom** page, and Odyssey **Ai-ONE Milled Rossie V T DB** page checked on **July 26, 2026**, plus Birdie Report's surrounding premium-putter cluster. No fake "I rented a private putting bay and found enlightenment" nonsense.

## Quick Verdict

Buy the **L.A.B. DF3** if your biggest issue is face control and start line, and you want the stronger "please stop the face from doing dumb stuff" thesis.

Buy the **Odyssey Ai-ONE Milled Rossie V T** if you want the easier same-price premium-mallet recommendation because the shape is more familiar, the setup picture is calmer, and the whole help-first story is easier to trust quickly.

The **DF3** is the bigger swing.

The **Rossie** is the easier buy.

## Why This Comparison Matters

A lot of premium-putter comparisons are fake forks.

This one is not.

At the current prices, these two are competing for golfers who want:

- a premium mallet
- real help, not just nicer milling
- a coherent reason to spend around **$400**

But they get there in very different ways.

The **DF3** says:

"Let me attack face behavior directly."

The **Rossie** says:

"Let me give you a familiar premium-mallet setup and protect your misses without turning the whole purchase into a science project."

That is a real decision tree.

## Face Control and Start Line: DF3 Has the Stronger Thesis

This is where the **DF3** earns its place.

L.A.B.'s entire pitch is about Lie Angle Balance and reducing the amount of face babysitting your hands need to do through the stroke.

That is a sharper performance claim than:

- better finish
- nicer feel
- louder alignment bars

If you are the golfer who keeps missing because the ball starts just enough offline to ruin the putt, the **DF3** is built to sound convincing.

That is also why the full [L.A.B. DF3 review](/blog/lab-df3-putter-review/) still matters before you buy. The product is unusual, but the problem it is trying to solve is not unusual at all.

If the face is the issue, the **DF3 wins this section**.

## Familiarity and Setup Comfort: Rossie Wins the Easier-Buy Argument

The **Rossie V T** is not trying to reinvent the act of putting.

It is trying to take the current **Ai-ONE Milled** premium-help thesis and package it in a shape that feels calmer than the **Jailbird Mini T**.

That matters a lot.

Some golfers want help but do not want to feel like their putter needs a TED Talk before it makes sense.

Odyssey's current page describes the Rossie as a blend of **Rossie** and **V-Line** shapes with rounded bumpers and a classic mallet size. That is basically Odyssey admitting this branch is for golfers who still want a premium mallet to look approachable.

If your goal is:

- same-money premium help
- less visual weirdness
- easier setup comfort

the **Rossie V T** has the cleaner blind recommendation.

## Feel and Ownership Vibe

Neither of these is a nostalgia purchase.

But they still scratch different ownership itches.

The **DF3** feels like you bought an engineering answer.

The **Rossie** feels like you bought a premium mallet that still understands golfers care about setup comfort, shape, and not looking like they joined a cult.

That difference matters more than putter nerds like admitting.

If you want the product with the more disruptive identity, the **DF3** wins.

If you want the product you can understand in ten seconds without needing to become ideologically pro-zero-torque, the **Rossie** wins.
