Myrtle Beach gives golfers an unusual problem: the hard part is not finding a tee time. It is deciding which courses deserve a limited number of rounds.

This guide narrows the Grand Strand to the courses that create the strongest first-trip itinerary. It is based on current official course information, architecture, setting, access, and how the stops fit together—not a claim that we personally replayed every course this season.

The short answer:

1. **Caledonia Golf & Fish Club** is the priority round.
2. **True Blue Golf Club** is the natural second half of a Mike Strantz day.
3. **Tidewater Golf Club** gives the trip a different coastal setting.
4. **The Dunes Golf & Beach Club** belongs on the list when limited guest access cooperates.
5. **Heritage Club** is a useful lower-pressure complement to the bigger-name rounds.

Rates change by date, demand, and time of day. Use the live booking pages before treating any trip budget as final.

## 1. Caledonia Golf & Fish Club

Caledonia is the cleanest first booking because the setting and design give the trip an identity immediately. The official course describes the live-oak entrance, rice-field property, and Mike Strantz design; those are more useful reasons to prioritize it than an unsupported promise about a specific green speed or conditioning level.

The practical question is price. Caledonia publishes seasonal rates rather than one permanent number. For example, its [official 2026 summer schedule](https://caledoniagolfandfishclub.com/rates-specials/) listed **$129 morning, $99 afternoon, and $79 twilight** from June 22 through August 30, with rates subject to change. That is a snapshot, not a year-round promise.

**Best fit:** the first premium round of the trip.

**Planning move:** compare the live single-round price with any Caledonia/True Blue combination available for your dates.

## 2. True Blue Golf Club

True Blue is not just “the other course next to Caledonia.” The official course details list a Mike Strantz design that opened in 1998, stretches to 7,126 yards, offers six tee sets, and includes an 18-acre practice facility.

That combination makes it useful for a group with mixed handicaps. The course can still ask difficult strategic questions, but the tee options give the organizer a better chance of finding a fair setup for everyone.

The same official summer 2026 schedule listed **$109 morning, $89 afternoon, and $59 twilight** for the June 22–August 30 window. Again, use the live rate for your exact date.

**Best fit:** a second round after Caledonia, or the better standalone choice for a group that wants a bolder and roomier visual test.

## 3. Tidewater Golf Club

Tidewater creates contrast. Its official site places the course between the Intracoastal Waterway and Cherry Grove Inlet and says nine holes run along the tidal inlet. That is a strong reason to include it after a day spent farther south around Pawleys Island.

Tidewater is a public daily-fee course, and its FAQ directs golfers to the online tee sheet for current rates and availability. That is better planning information than a fixed price range that may be stale before the trip begins.

**Best fit:** the scenic anchor for a north-end day.

**Planning move:** do not schedule Tidewater and a Pawleys Island course back-to-back without checking drive time. Myrtle Beach looks compact on a map, but the Grand Strand itinerary can sprawl quickly.

## 4. The Dunes Golf & Beach Club

The Dunes adds history and a traditional club environment to the trip. The club identifies it as a Robert Trent Jones course, and the current access page says it remains private but offers a limited number of guest tee times depending on the day and time.

That is an important correction to the idea that golfers always need a member invitation or a resort package. Limited unaccompanied guest inventory can appear through the club's booking engine, but availability restrictions apply and unaccompanied guests are not permitted on Fridays, Saturdays, or holiday weekends.

**Best fit:** golfers who value architecture and history enough to build the schedule around limited access.

**Planning move:** check the official guest calendar before arranging the rest of that day.

## 5. Heritage Club

Heritage works because every trip does not need to be five straight attempts at a marquee name. The official course page emphasizes its Lowcountry setting and live oaks, and its location south of Myrtle Beach makes it a logical pairing with a Pawleys Island base.

It is the type of round that can balance an itinerary: play the harder-to-book priority courses first, then use Heritage when the group needs a more relaxed final morning or a schedule-friendly fourth round.

**Best fit:** the third-day close or the value slot in a four-round trip.

## What About Barefoot, Pawleys Plantation, and TPC Myrtle Beach?

They remain legitimate trip options. They are not on this five-course priority list because the goal here is to make a first trip coherent, not to declare every course outside the top five “overrated.”

- Choose **Barefoot Resort** when the group wants multiple courses and lodging in one northern hub.
- Choose **Pawleys Plantation** when a Jack Nicklaus design and a southern Grand Strand base fit the group.
- Choose **TPC Myrtle Beach** when its live price, location, and tournament-style presentation beat the alternatives for your dates.

The previous version of this guide made overly absolute skip recommendations and used fixed prices as if Myrtle Beach tee sheets never moved. That is not a useful way to plan a demand-priced golf market.

## A Better Three-Day Myrtle Beach Itinerary

### Day 1: The Strantz pairing

- Morning: Caledonia
- Afternoon: True Blue, if the group is comfortable with 36 holes
- Alternative: play one and use the other as the next morning's round

### Day 2: Change the scenery

- Morning: Tidewater
- Afternoon: a north-end course or an early finish

### Day 3: Match the group's energy

- History-first option: The Dunes, if a guest time is available
- Lower-pressure option: Heritage Club
- Resort-convenience option: one of the Barefoot courses

This itinerary avoids fake precision about a total golf bill. Calculate the real number from the live tee sheets, then add lodging, ground transportation, food, and any replay round.

## When to Go

Spring and fall are the conventional trip windows because temperatures are generally easier for 36-hole days, but they also attract demand. Summer can produce lower promotional rates alongside heat and humidity. Winter can offer value, but weather and turf transitions add variability.

The best month is the one where the group's tolerance for heat, crowds, and price lines up. Before paying deposits, check:

- the course's current maintenance and aerification notices
- cancellation and rain-check rules
- whether a quoted rate includes a cart
- the drive between lodging and both tee times
- sunset if booking an afternoon or twilight round

## Where to Stay

Choose lodging after choosing the core courses.

- **Pawleys Island or Murrells Inlet:** best for Caledonia, True Blue, Heritage, and other south-end rounds.
- **Central Myrtle Beach:** the compromise when the course list spans both ends.
- **North Myrtle Beach:** best when Tidewater, Barefoot, or other north-end courses dominate the itinerary.

Staying north while playing every premium round south can turn a golf trip into a traffic trip.

## The Bottom Line

For a first Myrtle Beach golf trip, build around **Caledonia, True Blue, and Tidewater**. Add **The Dunes** when guest access works, then use **Heritage** or another convenient public course to keep the schedule and budget sane.
