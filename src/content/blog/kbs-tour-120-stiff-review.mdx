**I play KBS Tour 120 stiff because I like the heavier feel, the feedback, and the way that combination helps me control trajectory.** That makes it a shaft I would put on a fitting shortlist. It does not make it an automatic recommendation for another golfer's irons.

The useful question is whether that weight and feel help you deliver the club. Buying my shaft because you want my preference is an expensive way to skip your own fitting.

## What This Review Is Based On

The first-hand observations here come from my existing playing experience, also described in [Steel vs Graphite Iron Shafts](/blog/steel-vs-graphite-iron-shafts/). The product specifications below were checked against [KBS's TOUR specification page](https://kbsgolfshafts.com/products/tour) on September 23, 2026.

This is an account of my shaft choice, not a new controlled test. I am not publishing a measured distance gain, spin reduction, dispersion percentage, or swing-speed cutoff. I do not have a comparison dataset attached to this review that would support those promises.

## My Verdict: Start With Feel, Then Check the Result

The combination of weight and feedback is why I stay with this shaft. It suits my preference for a heavier feel and helps me control trajectory.

Those are personal observations. They are not proof that more weight improves everyone's timing. If your current irons become hard work late in a round, copying a heavier build without testing it makes very little sense.

I have also hit graphite iron shafts on a launch monitor that produced comparable or better numbers. Playing steel is my preference, not evidence that graphite loses. The broader [steel-versus-graphite guide](/blog/steel-vs-graphite-iron-shafts/) covers that decision; this page is specifically about the KBS Tour 120 stiff option.

## Check the Tip Before You Buy

This is the specification detail I would check before spending a cent. KBS lists different weights for the taper and parallel versions of TOUR. A listing that says “KBS Tour stiff” does not settle the question.

| KBS TOUR version | Published weight | Tip diameter |
|---|---:|---:|
| Taper, stiff | 120g | .355 inch |
| Parallel, stiff | 130g | .370 inch |

Those are KBS's specifications, not measurements of my installed shafts. KBS describes the parallel version as a single-length shaft that requires tip and butt trimming. Have the builder confirm the correct component and preparation for the head. Do not assume two listings are interchangeable because the flex word matches.

## Who I Would Put It on a Shortlist For

I would start here if you already like a substantial steel-shaft feel and want to compare a complete build against what you play now. Bring the current club to the fitting so there is an actual reference point.

My questions would be:

- Does the weight make the club easier to control, or does it make the swing feel like work?
- Does the strike pattern stay usable when the session is no longer fresh?
- Can you produce the carry window and landing flight you need?
- Do you still like the feedback on imperfect contact?

If it only wins when you look at the brand label, keep testing. A good fitting result is more useful than a shaft collection.

## Who Should Keep Comparing

Golfers looking primarily to reduce overall club weight should include lighter options rather than assuming stiff flex requires this weight. Golfers who dislike the impact sensation of their current steel build should also test other constructions.

That is a fitting suggestion, not a claim that every lightweight or graphite shaft will feel better. The iron head, length, grip and complete build matter too. Keep those changes visible when comparing results instead of crediting the shaft for everything.

## Buying Checklist

Before ordering a replacement or a full set, confirm the exact TOUR model, flex, tip size and intended iron with the builder. Check whether the listing is for one shaft or a set, and whether a used shaft has already been cut or pulled from another head. Include installation, grips and any required build checks when comparing the total cost.
