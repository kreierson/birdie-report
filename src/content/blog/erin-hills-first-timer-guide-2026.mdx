There is a very specific way to waste an Erin Hills round.

You show up to one of the biggest walking tests in public golf, get seduced by all the width, tell yourself the place is more generous than the U.S. Open reputation suggests, and then spend nearly five hours discovering that wide is not the same thing as easy.

That is the dumb version.

This is not a fake firsthand diary where I pretend I just came off property with secret wind notes and a spiritual attachment to every rumple. This is a practical first-timer guide built from Erin Hills' current official **course**, **rates**, **FAQs**, **caddie**, and **scorecard** pages checked on **Friday, August 7, 2026**.

If you want the bigger worth-it verdict first, start with [our Erin Hills review](/blog/erin-hills-review/) and [best golf courses in Wisconsin](/blog/best-golf-courses-wisconsin/). This page is for the golfer who already knows Erin Hills is on the trip sheet and wants the cleanest plan for not turning the day into long-walk cleanup.

## Quick Answer

For most first-timers, the smart Erin Hills plan is:

- treat it like a **full walking day**, not a casual premium tee time
- assume the round is going to take close to **five hours**
- either budget for a **caddie** or be honest about what you lose by skipping one
- play from a tee that keeps you in the **6,252- to 6,537-yard** neighborhood unless you have real carry speed
- and spend the whole day avoiding the high-side, short-sided, fake-aggressive miss

That is the adult version.

## First Thing: This Is a Walking Day, Not a Cart Day

Erin Hills' current FAQ is very direct:

- **Erin Hills is walking only**
- **outside caddies and pull-carts are not allowed**
- and the course asks you to plan on a **four hour and fifty-five minute round**

That matters more than the marketing poetry.

This is not the place to show up under-trained, under-hydrated, and weirdly confident that "I walk plenty" because you once strolled nine holes at your home course with a push cart and a transfusion.

Erin Hills is a real property.
Treat it like one.

If your shoes are questionable, fix that before the trip with [best golf shoes for walking 2026](/blog/best-golf-shoes-walking-2026/) and [best golf shoes 2026](/blog/best-golf-shoes-2026/).

## Second Thing: No Twilight Means the Whole Day Is the Point

Erin Hills' FAQ says the course does **not offer twilight tee times** because management wants guests to experience the entire golf course.

That is actually useful trip-planning information.

It means this round is supposed to be the event, not the late-afternoon add-on you wedge between breakfast bravado and dinner reservations.

Build your day around it.

### What that means in practice

- eat before you start
- warm up properly
- carry more water than you think you need
- and do not book yourself into dumb post-round time pressure

The course already has enough ways to make you feel rushed. You do not need to bring your own.

## The Caddie Math Is Real, but So Is the Value

Erin Hills' current rates page lists:

- **$65 per player** caddie service fee
- **$75+ recommended gratuity** per player
- **$65 per group** forecaddie fee
- **$35+ suggested gratuity** per player for a forecaddie

That is real money.

It is also one of the easiest premium-golf charges to justify if this is your first time around a giant walking property with firm turf, massive greens, and a bunch of recovery shots that get expensive when you start guessing.

My first-timer advice:

- take a **full caddie** if this is your one-shot Erin Hills trip
- take a **forecaddie** if the group wants help without paying for the full deluxe version
- carry your own bag only if long walking rounds are already normal for you and you are not pretending

Erin Hills also rents the current **TaylorMade** setup for **$85 per round**, so if you are flying in and do not want baggage nonsense, that is at least a clean, current number.

## Tee Choice: Be Honest Before the Course Does It for You

Erin Hills' current scorecard lists these total yardages:

- **Blue: 7,357**
- **Green/White combo: 6,537**
- **White: 6,252**
- **White/Gold combo: 5,670**

The macho mistake is obvious.

You see the width, remember the place hosted a U.S. Open, and convince yourself you should "get the full experience" from farther back.

That is how you buy a lot of long-iron stress on a course that is already asking enough.

My practical first-timer rule:

- if your normal drive is more like **225 to 240**, start at **White**
- if you are a stronger player with enough carry to still keep approaches sane, **Green/White** is the sharper first-visit test
- if you need **Blue** to feel validated, be honest about whether you are chasing golf or cosplay

That tee advice is my inference from the current scorecard and the shape of the course, not a direct Erin Hills instruction. It is still the smarter play for most people.

## The Smart Way to Think About the Green Fee

Erin Hills' current 2026 rates page says:

- **$495** from **May 26 through September 27**
- **$395** from **May 4 through May 25** and **September 28 through October 17**
- **$295** for an **18-hole replay**, booked the same day and based on availability

If you are staying overnight during those shoulder windows, Erin Hills also lists a **$200 second-round fee** for stay-and-play guests.

That changes the trip math a lot.

There is also the **Kettle Loop** wrinkle. Erin Hills says there is **no caddie or green fee** for the Kettle Loop because it is reserved for overnight guests, by request and pending availability.

So the cleanest first-timer version is not "fly in, walk 18, and sprint home."

The cleaner version is:

- book the shoulder-season window if you can
- stay on property if the budget allows
- and use the second-round / Kettle Loop access to spread the travel pain over more golf

## The No-Hero Script for the Round

This is the sentence I would write on the card:

**At Erin Hills, your bad miss cannot also be proud.**

The width invites you to feel bold.
The greens punish you for being dumb from the wrong side.

So the whole day is about discipline.

### 1. Through six holes, your goal is no doubles

That is it.

Not "I need to attack while I am fresh."
Not "I can take advantage before the wind gets up."

Just:

- keep the ball in front of you
- keep penalties off the card
- and let the course show you how far the ball is really releasing

If you are **+3 or better through 6 with no doubles**, you are doing fine.

### 2. Play to the fat side and make the putter work

Erin Hills' course page talks about firm playing surfaces and a layout that can play shorter than the raw scorecard because of the ground.

That is fun off the tee.
It is less fun when you short-side yourself on or around those greens.

This is the exact kind of place where [how to play firm greens without short-siding yourself](/blog/how-to-play-firm-greens-without-short-siding-yourself/) stops sounding like generic advice and starts saving real shots.

### 3. On long par 4s, stop trying to force "proper" golf

If you are outside your comfort window, play for the easiest bogey on the property.

That usually means:

- front-edge or center-green target
- no miracle carry decision
- and no emotional extra club just because the hole looks big and expensive

If you need that mindset reset, read [how to play long par 4s without making doubles](/blog/how-to-play-long-par-4s-without-making-doubles/) and [how to save a round when driver is gone](/blog/how-to-save-a-round-when-driver-is-gone/).

### 4. On par 5s, lay up to a number you actually own

I would much rather have:

- **95 to 110 yards**
- or **115 to 130 yards**

...than some brave garbage from a half-fit lie on a first visit.

If that decision tree needs work, go back through [when to go for par 5s in two](/blog/when-to-go-for-par-5s-in-two/).

## The One Practice Block I Would Do Before the Trip

If you only get one prep session before Erin Hills, make it this:

### The 9-ball lag ladder

Hit:

- **3 putts from 30 feet**
- **3 putts from 40 feet**
- **3 putts from 50 feet**

Rules:

- every ball has to finish inside **3 feet**
- if more than **2 of 9** finish outside that circle, start over

Why this drill?

Because a first Erin Hills round gets a lot less scary when your first putt is allowed to be boring.

## Bottom Line

For a first-timer, **Erin Hills is not mainly a courage test. It is a planning test.**

The current official pages checked on **Friday, August 7, 2026** make the shape of the day pretty clear:

- it is **walking only**
- it is basically a **five-hour round**
- it does **not** do twilight
- the caddie math is real
- and the scorecard gives you a clean adult lane at **White (6,252)** or **Green/White (6,537)** if you are honest

Pick the right tee. Budget the walking. Stop trying to win the trip on every long approach.

Do that, and Erin Hills looks like the kind of first round you remember for the golf instead of for the cleanup.
