## Quick Verdict

The **Precision Pro NX10** is the better buy for most golfers because it handles the actual day-to-day rangefinder job for less than half the price.

The **Garmin Approach Z82** is the more interesting product because it adds:

- full-color **CourseView** maps in the viewfinder
- **Laser Range Arc**
- **Green View**
- **PinPointer**
- the whole "laser plus GPS strategy layer" idea

That is real differentiation.

It is also not something most golfers need badly enough to justify a **$320** jump.

## Why the NX10 Wins for Most Golfers

The **NX10** wins because it is closer to how most golfers actually use a rangefinder:

- range the flag
- trust the number
- maybe use slope
- hit the shot

Precision Pro's current product page keeps the pitch very practical:

- **magnetic cart mount**
- **20-plus interchangeable side and face plates**
- **slope**
- **tournament-legal slope switch**
- **6x magnification**
- **400-yard** flag lock
- **IP67** waterproofing
- a replaceable **CR2** battery that Precision Pro says lasts about **50 rounds**

That is enough product for a lot of golfers.

And at **$279.99**, it is enough product without making the whole purchase feel slightly stupid.

## Why the Z82 Still Has a Real Case

The **Z82** is not just trying to be a better laser.

It is trying to collapse two devices into one:

- your rangefinder
- your GPS course-context tool

Garmin's current product page still gives it a legitimately different feature stack:

- more than **41,000** preloaded courses
- **full color CourseView** maps overlaid in the viewfinder
- **Laser Range Arc**
- **Green View**
- **PinPointer**
- **image stabilization**
- **vibrational feedback**
- **450-yard** laser range to the flag
- **up to 15 hours** of battery life in GPS mode

That is not fake differentiation.

It is just expensive differentiation.

If you are the kind of golfer who is already using a GPS watch or handheld every round and you are tired of the two-device dance, the **Z82** makes more sense than it does on a simple price sheet.

If you are not that golfer, it starts looking like a very clever way to overbuy.

## This Is Really Workflow Versus Gadget Ambition

That is the cleanest way to frame it.

The **NX10** is the easier answer if you want a rangefinder to stay in its lane.

The **Z82** is the better answer if you actively want your rangefinder to give you more context around:

- what else sits at that distance
- what the green looks like
- where the blind-shot direction actually is

That can be useful.

It can also be a lot of extra theater if your normal process is already working.

## Price Gap: This Is Where the Garmin Has to Work Hard

At current official pricing checked on **July 26, 2026**:

- **Garmin Approach Z82:** **$599.99**
- **Precision Pro NX10:** **$279.99**

That is not a small gap.

So Garmin does not just need to be more interesting.

It needs to be meaningfully more useful.

For some golfers, it will be.

For most golfers, I do not think it is.
