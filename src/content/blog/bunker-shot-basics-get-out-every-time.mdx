A standard greenside bunker shot is an **explosion shot**: the club enters the sand behind the ball, travels under it, and sends a layer of sand—and the ball—onto the green.

That sounds simple, but “hit two inches behind it” is not a complete technique. The amount and texture of sand, the lie, the lip, and the distance to the pin all change the setup.

This page covers the stock greenside shot first, then shows you what to adjust.

## The Standard Greenside Bunker Checklist

Use this for a normal lie in medium-depth sand with enough green to work with.

1. **Choose a sand or lob wedge with usable bounce.**
2. **Open the face before taking your final grip.**
3. **Set the ball forward of center.**
4. **Dig your feet in enough to create a stable stance.**
5. **Favor the lead side and keep your chest centered.**
6. **Aim the body slightly left of the target for a right-handed golfer.**
7. **Swing through the sand and finish—do not stop at impact.**

The goal is not to lift the ball. The loft and sand do that. Your job is to deliver the sole of the club through the sand with enough speed.

## Why Opening the Face Matters

Opening the face adds loft, but the more important effect is exposing the sole's **bounce**. Bounce helps the club resist digging too deeply.

Open the face first, then place your hands on the grip. If you grip a square face and twist it open only with your hands, it is easier to return the face to a different position at impact.

The face does not need to look horizontal. Start modestly open and add loft only when the lip, landing spot, and sand allow it.

## Ball Position and Entry Point

For the stock shot, play the ball inside the lead heel or a ball forward of center. A forward ball position moves the intended entry point behind the ball.

Use a **wide entry window**, not a microscopic spot:

- standard sand: enter roughly one to two inches behind the ball,
- very soft sand: a slightly wider patch can work,
- firm or shallow sand: enter closer to the ball with less open face.

Trying to stab an exact grain of sand creates tension. Think about sending a shallow strip of sand onto the green.

## How Hard Should You Swing?

The sand absorbs speed, so the motion must be larger than a chip from the same distance. But “make a 50-yard swing” is not universal; deep fluffy sand absorbs more energy than a shallow compact lie.

Calibrate with this rule:

- more sand and more open face require more speed,
- less sand and a squarer face require less speed,
- a longer shot needs either less loft, less sand, more speed, or a combination.

Never solve distance by decelerating.

## Adjust for the Sand You Actually Have

Digging your feet in to take a stance is allowed under [USGA Rule 12](https://www.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/custom-search-pages/rules/2019-golf-rules-and-interpretations/fr-rule-12.html), and the resistance under your shoes gives useful information. Do not deliberately test the sand with a hand or club, and do not touch it with the club immediately in front of or behind the ball, during a practice swing, or in the backswing.

| Condition | Clubface | Ball position | Entry and motion | Expected flight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Soft, deep sand | more open | forward | use bounce, wider splash, maintain speed | higher, softer |
| Medium sand | modestly open | forward of center | stock one-to-two-inch entry | stock explosion |
| Firm, compact sand | less open | near center-forward | enter closer, shallower motion | lower, more release |
| Wet, shallow sand | less open | near center | minimal sand, controlled speed | lower and faster |
| Buried “fried egg” | square or slightly closed | center | steeper entry, drive through surrounding sand | low, little spin |

The wet/compact version deserves its own treatment; use [greenside bunker shots from wet compact sand](/blog/how-to-hit-greenside-bunker-shots-from-wet-compact-sand/) when the sole keeps bouncing into the ball.

## Three Greenside Distances

### Short shot with little green

Use more loft and a fuller finish only when the lie is clean and enough sand sits under the ball. The safe target may still be beyond the flag if the lip or lie makes a delicate shot unrealistic.

For the full decision process, read [short-sided greenside bunker shots](/blog/short-sided-greenside-bunker-shots/).

### Standard shot

Use the stock checklist: open face, ball forward, stable lead-side pressure, and a complete motion through a consistent strip of sand.

### Long greenside bunker shot

Use less open face, a ball position closer to center, and a narrower strip of sand. The shot should launch lower and release.

Do not turn it into a fairway-bunker iron shot. Our [20–40 yard bunker guide](/blog/long-greenside-bunker-shots-20-to-40-yards/) covers the landing-window choices for that awkward distance.

## Plugged and Buried Lies

A buried lie changes the job. You are no longer trying to slide the bounce under the ball.

1. Square or slightly close the face.
2. Move the ball toward center.
3. Put pressure on the lead foot.
4. Make a steeper, committed strike into the sand near the ball.
5. Expect a low shot with very little spin.

The goal is getting onto a safe part of the green. Trying to manufacture a soft, spinning finish from a buried lie is how the ball stays in the bunker.

See [how to hit buried bunker lies](/blog/how-to-hit-buried-bunker-lies/) for the complete escape plan.

## This Is Not a Fairway-Bunker Technique

Greenside bunker shots and fairway bunker shots have opposite contact goals:

| Shot | Contact goal | Typical face | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greenside bunker | sand before ball | open | use bounce and control landing |
| Fairway bunker | ball before sand | square | clear the lip and advance safely |

If the ball is 120 yards from the green, do not use this explosion setup. Start with the [fairway-bunker contact guide](/blog/how-to-hit-fairway-bunker-shots-cleanly/) and use the [go-or-chip-out checks](/blog/fairway-bunker-shots-go-or-chip-out/) before choosing the club.

## Troubleshooting Bunker Misses

| Miss | Likely cause | First correction |
|---|---|---|
| Ball stays in bunker | deceleration, too much sand, or face too open | finish the swing; narrow the sand strip |
| Ball blades over green | ball-first contact or too much bounce on firm sand | enter closer with less open face |
| Shot flies too far | too little sand or face too square | open slightly and use a wider splash |
| Shot comes out heavy and short | club digs under the sand | expose more bounce and keep speed |
| Shot starts right | face excessively open or body never rotates | reduce face opening and finish chest through |
| Inconsistent entry point | pressure shifts back or swing bottoms out early | keep chest centered over lead side |

## The Line Drill

The most useful bunker drill does not need a target.

1. Draw a line in the practice bunker perpendicular to the target line.
2. Take five swings with no ball and enter the sand on the line.
3. Place five balls about one inch ahead of the line.
4. Score one point each time the entry begins on or just behind the line and the club exits forward.
5. Repeat from firmer and softer sections if the practice area allows.

Do not touch the sand with a practice swing during a round. This drill belongs in a designated practice bunker.

## The Landing-Zone Drill

Once contact is predictable:

1. Place towels or targets at short, medium, and long landing zones.
2. Hit three balls to each.
3. Change only one variable at a time: face angle, entry width, or swing length.
4. Record which combination creates each carry window.

That gives you a bunker system rather than one swing that works only from one texture.

## The Bottom Line

For the stock greenside bunker shot:

- open the face enough to expose bounce,
- play the ball forward,
- create a stable stance,
- enter the sand behind the ball,
- and keep the club moving through the sand.

Then adjust to the condition. Soft sand needs more bounce and speed; firm sand needs less face opening and a narrower strike; buried lies need a steeper escape.
