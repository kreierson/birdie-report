Team golf has a bad habit of turning into future-casting theater.

A young player wins once, everybody gets stars in their eyes, and suddenly the argument becomes less about **who gives you the best 12 for this week** and more about who might be awesome in **2028**.

That is how you end up talking yourself into cute roster decisions.

According to the **PGA TOUR's August 4, 2026 report** on **Brandt Snedeker's** current **Presidents Cup** thinking, checked on **Wednesday, August 5**, the U.S. captain said there are **"probably 20, 22 guys"** with a chance to make the team right now. The same report says Snedeker has no interest in manufacturing a youth slot just because a young player might become a long-term fixture later.

That is the correct answer.

This column is based on the PGA TOUR's August 4 Presidents Cup roster story, checked on August 5, 2026. No pretending I got a private Medinah whiteboard tour with lineup magnets already color-coded.

For the current pressure context, read [our Wyndham column on why this week is supposed to feel cruel](/blog/wyndham-championship-cruel-week-opinion-2026/), [our news recap of Jackson Koivun's 3M Open win](/blog/jackson-koivun-wins-3m-open-third-pro-start-2026/), and the earlier [Brandt Snedeker feel-good run at Valspar](/blog/brandt-snedeker-45-sponsor-exemption-valspar/).

## The Koivun Question Is Real, but the Principle Matters More

This whole conversation came up because **Jackson Koivun** is no longer a theoretical talent story.

He already beat **Scottie Scheffler** at the **3M Open**.
He is sitting right on the **FedExCup Playoffs bubble** entering the **Wyndham Championship**.
He is obviously one of the most interesting young Americans in golf.

So yes, the question is fair.

Should a player like that get a little extra team-event runway because he looks like a future star?

Snedeker's answer, per the TOUR's report, was basically no:
the golf decides.

Perfect.

## "Best 12 Right Now" Is Not Boring. It Is the Job.

Snedeker's logic is straightforward.

The TOUR report says he wants players to **prove it under pressure**, and when asked directly about bringing in **"new, young blood,"** he said the team should be built around the players who give the U.S. the **best chance to win**.

That should not be controversial.

The Presidents Cup is not a fellowship program.
It is not a rookie orientation week.
It is not a content series about the next generation.

It is a competition at **Medinah Country Club**, and the U.S. team has enough talent that fake developmental charity should not enter the equation.

## This Does Not Hurt Koivun. It Helps Keep the Standard Honest.

The funny part is that this stance does not shut the door on Koivun at all.

It just means he has to earn it the hard way.

And honestly, that is better for him too.

If Koivun plays his way onto the team, nobody has to spend the week doing the annoying little "is he here for now or for later?" dance. He is just there because his golf made the decision unavoidable.

That is cleaner for:

- Koivun
- Snedeker
- the locker room
- and the entire structure of the pick process

The current PGA TOUR story notes that Koivun is sitting at **No. 70 in the FedExCup**, the last projected playoff spot. That makes the next stretch easy to understand. If he wants the conversation to become unavoidable, he has actual golf to play first.

Good.

## Golf Has Enough Nostalgia and Enough Projection Already

The U.S. side does not need to create another trap where fans treat every team roster like a draft of future marketable stars.

It especially does not need that in a year where Snedeker says roughly **20 to 22** players are in the mix.

That is a healthy place for the roster discussion to be.

It means options.
It means depth.
It means the captain can wait and see who is actually sharp.

The dumbest possible move in that situation would be to talk yourself into a symbolic youth slot because it sounds visionary on television.

Vision is nice.
Points count more.

## My Take

If a young player is one of the best 12 Americans, take him.

If he is not, stop trying to turn the Presidents Cup into a TED Talk about the future of the game.

Snedeker's stance is useful because it trims away a lot of noise. Team-event rosters always attract overthinking, and overthinking usually arrives dressed as sophistication. People start talking about momentum, chemistry mythology, messaging, legacy, and what a pick "means."

What it should mean is very simple:

this guy helps us win at Medinah.

That is it.

## Bottom Line

Checked on **Wednesday, August 5, 2026**, the **PGA TOUR** reports that **Brandt Snedeker** sees roughly **20 to 22 players** in the current **Presidents Cup** mix and does not want to force a young player onto the roster just for age balance or future vibes.

He is right.

If **Jackson Koivun** or any other young American earns one of the 12 spots, great.
If not, the U.S. team does not need a youth quota.
It needs the best lineup it can bring to **Medinah**.
