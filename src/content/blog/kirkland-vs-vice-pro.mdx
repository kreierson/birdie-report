Buy **Kirkland Signature** if you want the smarter receipt, the cheapest legitimate urethane-ball path in this comparison, and enough balls in one buy to stop acting weird every time one disappears into the trees.

Buy **Vice Pro** if you want the more refined product story, a clearer lower-spin long-game fit, and a value-premium DTC ball that still feels like a deliberate golf choice instead of a glorious Costco loophole.

For most golfers making this exact comparison, I would recommend **Kirkland Signature**.

For the golfer who specifically wants Vice's cleaner lower-spin all-rounder fit and does not mind paying materially more per ball to get it, I would recommend **Vice Pro**.

## Flight and Fit: Vice Has the Sharper Long-Game Identity

This is where **Vice Pro** gets interesting.

Vice explicitly says the Pro is engineered for:

- lower spin with **woods and long irons**
- a slightly **flatter trajectory**
- more roll and distance on long shots

That is a real differentiator.

Kirkland's official product language is broader and more generic. It is about value, urethane cover, greenside control, and overall playability.

Vice's language is more targeted.

So if you are the golfer who already knows you hate excess driver spin and you want a value-urethane ball with a more specific long-game flight story, **Vice Pro** has the better case.

If you are not that golfer, Kirkland's broader "cheap real urethane ball" appeal is still the smarter default.

## Short-Game Story: Both Are Legit, Just in Different Ways

Neither of these is a fake-premium two-piece ball pretending to be something else.

The **Kirkland** product story is still built around urethane, iron spin, and greenside control.

The **Vice Pro** page gets more specific:

- the **thinnest cast urethane cover**
- maximum control and **short-game spin**
- pure and balanced feedback

That does not mean Kirkland suddenly stops making sense around the greens.

It just means Vice tells a cleaner "this is a real purpose-built all-rounder" story, while Kirkland tells a louder "look what this costs" story.

For a lot of golfers, the cost story is enough.

For golfers who are more particular about feel and long-game fit, it may not be.
