## How to read this list

This is a research shortlist, not a personal putting trial. The ranking follows published head shapes, balance, and the job each putter is built for: a blade for an arcing stroke, a mallet for face balance and a higher miss floor, a mid-mallet when you want both. Feel still has to be decided on a green. A page cannot tell you which one you will trust over a four-footer.

## 1. Scotty Cameron Super Select Newport 2 — The Best Putter in Golf

**Rating: 9.5/10**

Yeah, I know. Scotty Cameron at the top of a putter list — groundbreaking stuff. But there's a reason every putter company is still chasing this design 30+ years later. The Super Select Newport 2 is the most refined version of an already iconic shape.

The milling is the reason this head stays the reference. Cameron's published case is immediate feedback: a center strike and a miss are supposed to feel different, in 303 stainless. This page did not roll putts to confirm that feel.

**What makes it #1:** The alignment is the topline. No extra lines, dots, or sight gadgets. The design asks the player to aim the head, which is why it stays the reference blade even though a miss gets less help than a mallet.

**Who it's NOT for:** If you struggle with consistency and need forgiveness, a blade is going to expose you. Mishits lose energy fast, and there's no alignment aid to bail you out. If you're above a 10 handicap, keep reading.

If your actual premium-blade fork is **classic Scotty benchmark** versus **softer-feeling Ping craftsmanship**, the direct [Ping PLD Milled Anser vs Scotty Cameron Newport 2](/blog/scotty-cameron-vs-odyssey-putters/#ping-pld-milled-anser-vs-scotty-cameron-newport-2) page is the better next read than guessing from product-page vibes.

## 2. Ping PLD Milled Anser — The Connoisseur's Choice

**Rating: 9.3/10**

If the Scotty is a perfectly aged bourbon, the Ping PLD Milled Anser is a single-origin pour-over coffee. It's for people who *care* about craft.

Ping's PLD line is their premium, fully milled offering, and the Anser shape is arguably the most important putter design in history. This version is machined from a single block of 303 stainless steel and the tolerances are insane. Every surface is intentional.

The published feel contrast with the Newport 2 is a softer response and less of the firmer click. Which one a player prefers still has to be decided on a green. Slower greens are the usual reason to lean toward the softer Ping. Faster greens are the usual reason to lean toward the firmer Cameron. This page did not make that choice in a putting trial.

**The catch:** This is the premium end of the list, and it is harder to find than the Scotty. Ping doesn't mass-produce these, which is part of the appeal but also part of the frustration.

## 3. Odyssey Ai-ONE Milled Seven T — Best Mallet for 2026

**Rating: 9.2/10**

The Ai-ONE insert is Odyssey's published face-mapping idea: different textures in different zones, meant to keep ball speed more consistent when contact is off center. That consistency claim is why the mallet is on this research shortlist. It is not a strokes-gained result from this page.

The face pattern is the product. Odyssey's case is more consistent ball speed on a toe or heel miss than a milled blade gives up. For a mallet, that is the forgiveness argument.

The Seven T shape (that's the one with the single bend shaft and slight toe hang) suits a mild arc stroke. If you've got a straight-back-straight-through motion, look at the Seven S instead.

**Why it's not #1:** Feel. It's good — really good, actually — but it's an insert putter competing against milled steel. There's a "plasticky" quality on really soft putts that blade snobs (hi, that's me) will notice. Normal humans probably won't care.

## 4. Titleist Phantom X 5 — The Best Tweener

**Rating: 9.0/10**

The Phantom X 5 is for the golfer who looks at a blade and thinks "too small" but looks at a Spider and thinks "too big." It's a mid-mallet with a clean, almost futuristic look that inspires a shocking amount of confidence.

The multi-material construction (aluminum and steel) is Titleist's way of placing the center of gravity without making the head feel like an anchor. That stability is the published reason it suits a wide range of strokes. It is not a measured lag-putt result from this site.

**The truth:** This is the putter I'd recommend to the widest range of golfers. It's forgiving enough for a 15-handicap, precise enough for a scratch player, and it looks good doing it.

## 5. TaylorMade Spider GTx — The Forgiveness King

**Rating: 8.8/10**

The Spider has been the go-to "I need help on the greens" putter for years, and the GTx is the best version yet. The perimeter weighting is so aggressive that toe hits and heel hits come off nearly identical. If your miss pattern on putts is inconsistent contact, this is your putter.

It is a lot of putter at address. The case for it is the perimeter weighting: toe and heel contact keep more ball speed than they do on a blade, so the miss has a higher floor. That is a design argument, not a personal make-percentage.

**Best for:** Mid-to-high handicappers who need consistency more than feel. If you're 3-putting more than twice a round, the Spider will fix that faster than any lesson.

## 6. Cleveland Frontline Elevado — The Steal

**Rating: 8.5/10**

The Cleveland Frontline Elevado has the brand's forward-weighting system that reduces skid off the face and gets the ball rolling sooner. It's a real, published design — not a slogan. The price is why it is the value pick, not a measured make-percentage from this page.

Is it as refined as a Scotty? No. The finish isn't as pristine, the grip is just okay, and you won't get the same feedback a milled blade is known for. The forward-weighting system is why it still belongs on a shortlist that is otherwise much more expensive.

**Who should buy this:** Anyone who's been gaming a putter from a box set, anyone on a budget, or anyone who wants to spend their money on a driver and wedges instead. This punches way above its weight class.

## How to Choose the Right Putter

Forget what the tour pros use. Here's what actually matters:

1. **Stroke type:** Arc stroke → toe hang (blades, mid-mallets). Straight stroke → face-balanced (mallets). Get fitted if you're not sure.
2. **What you struggle with:** Direction? Get a putter with a strong alignment feature. Distance control? Look at insert putters or heavier heads. Confidence? Go with whatever looks good to YOUR eye.
3. **Green speed:** If you mostly play slow, shaggy munis, a heavier mallet helps. Fast, slick greens reward lighter, more responsive heads.
4. **Budget:** Diminishing returns show up once the putter is in the premium blade tier. The Cleveland is here because the forward-weighting system does a specific job. The Scotty is here because the shape and the milling are the reference. The last bit of feel is a preference, not a promise that one makes more putts.
