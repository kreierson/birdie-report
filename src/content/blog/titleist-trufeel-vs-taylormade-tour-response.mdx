The **Titleist TruFeel vs TaylorMade Tour Response** decision is where budget golf-ball shopping starts getting honest.

Because this is the fork where golfers stop asking, "What is soft and cheap?" and start asking:

"Do I stay in the value lane, or do I finally pay more for a urethane ball that might actually be worth it?"

That is a much better question.

This comparison is **research-based** and built from Titleist's official **2024 TruFeel launch details**, Titleist's **March 6, 2026** TruFeel AIM 360 release listing a **$24.99 MAP**, and TaylorMade's current **Tour Response** product positioning and **August 19, 2026** pricing check showing **$42.99** on the current product page. No fake on-course diary. No pretend "I tested both in a two-club wind and learned destiny" nonsense.

If you want the wider ball map first, start with [Best Golf Balls 2026](/blog/best-golf-balls-2026/), [Best Golf Balls for High Handicappers](/blog/best-golf-balls-high-handicappers/), the full [Titleist TruFeel review](/blog/titleist-trufeel-review/), and [TaylorMade Tour Response review](/blog/taylormade-tour-response-review/).

## Quick Verdict

Buy **TaylorMade Tour Response** if you want the smarter ball for most golfers who can justify the extra spend.

Buy **Titleist TruFeel** if you want to keep costs down, prefer a softer value ball with a lower and more penetrating flight, and still lose enough golf balls that a forty-dollar dozen feels annoying.

For most golfers who are starting to care more about short-game performance but still hate premium-ball pricing, I would lean **Tour Response**.

For golfers who mainly want a good soft ball and a calmer flight window, **TruFeel** still makes a real case.

## Why TruFeel Still Has a Real Job

The **TruFeel** case is not complicated.

It exists for golfers who want:

- a softer ball
- a more penetrating flight
- a familiar Titleist feel
- and a receipt that still looks normal

That last part matters.

At roughly **$25**, **TruFeel** is much easier to rebuy if you are still capable of donating balls to water, trees, and ego-driven tee shots.

It also has the sharper flight identity in this matchup. Titleist very directly frames it around **low, penetrating flight**, which makes it more interesting than a lot of cheap-soft options that only sell softness and vague distance promises.

If that sounds like your exact problem, the full [Titleist TruFeel review](/blog/titleist-trufeel-review/) is the next logical click.

## Why Tour Response Makes the Stronger Overall Case

This is where the page flips.

The **Tour Response** is not just more expensive for no reason.

TaylorMade gives it the features that justify the jump:

- **100% cast urethane cover**
- **3-layer construction**
- **Speed Wrapped Core**
- soft feel
- better short-game credibility than the cheap-soft category

That matters because this is the cheapest part of the market where you can still say, with a straight face, that the ball gives you a meaningful premium-material upgrade.

That is the whole argument for **Tour Response**.

It is not cheap-cheap.

It is smart-cheap for golfers who have realized they want a real urethane ball but still do not want to spend full flagship money.

That is exactly why the full [TaylorMade Tour Response review](/blog/taylormade-tour-response-review/) keeps fitting so naturally with the site's active value-urethane cluster.

## Price: TruFeel Wins Easily

This part is not close.

Using the current references above, **TruFeel** saves you about **$18 a dozen**.

That is a real gap.

If you:

- lose multiple balls a round
- practice with the same ball you play
- or just refuse to spend over forty bucks on a golf ball that is not a top-tier flagship

then **TruFeel** has the cleaner price story.

## Feel: Both Are Soft, but Tour Response Feels Like the Upgrade

This is not a brick-versus-pillow matchup.

Both of these balls are sold around soft feel.

The difference is in what the softness is attached to.

**TruFeel** feels like the softer value ball with a calmer flight.

**Tour Response** feels like the soft ball you buy when you want the softness to come with a more legitimate performance ceiling.

That matters.

Because plenty of golfers do not just want softness for comfort. They want softness plus the sense that they are finally playing a more complete product.

That is the **Tour Response** pitch in one sentence.

## Flight: TruFeel Has the Sharper Tee-Ball Identity

This is where **TruFeel** still wins something important.

Titleist gives it the more specific flight story in this matchup:

- lower
- more penetrating
- calmer for golfers who already launch it high enough

TaylorMade's **Tour Response** sounds more balanced and broader. That is good for the average golfer, but it is not the same as a targeted lower-flight fit.

So if the main thing you hate is watching soft balls climb too much, **TruFeel** has the cleaner answer.

**Edge: TruFeel**

## Around the Green: Tour Response Justifies the Price Here

This is the section that makes the upgrade case real.

The **urethane cover** is why the **Tour Response** exists.

Without it, the ball would just be another nice-sounding soft product in a crowded market.

With it, TaylorMade can make a much stronger case for:

- more credible greenside grab
- better touch on chips and pitches
- a more serious scoring-club profile than typical ionomer value balls

That does not mean **TruFeel** is bad around the greens. It is not.

It just means **Tour Response** has the feature that actually explains why it costs more.

If this is the category you care about most, the verdict is simple.

**Edge: Tour Response**

The **Titleist TruFeel** is the smarter buy if you want to keep your costs reasonable and you specifically like the idea of a softer ball with a lower, more penetrating flight.

The **TaylorMade Tour Response** is the smarter buy for most golfers who are ready to pay more for a ball that actually gives them a meaningful materials-and-short-game upgrade.

- **TruFeel for cheaper soft-ball sanity**
