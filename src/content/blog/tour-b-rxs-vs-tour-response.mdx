It is a choice between:

- the **premium moderate-speed ball with added-spin intent**
- and the **cheaper urethane ball that keeps telling you to stop spending like an idiot**

That is a real decision.

This comparison is **research-based** and built from Bridgestone Golf's current official **Tour B RXS** product page plus TaylorMade Golf's current official **Tour Response** product page, both checked on **Thursday, August 6, 2026**, alongside the active Birdie Report ball cluster that has already been pulling recent traffic into pages like [AVX vs Tour Response](/blog/avx-vs-tour-response/), [Chrome Soft vs Tour Response](/blog/chrome-soft-vs-tour-response/), and the broader [Best Golf Balls 2026](/blog/best-golf-balls-2026/) hub. Bridgestone still lists **Tour B RXS** at **$54.99 per dozen** and positions it for golfers swinging **under 105 mph** who want **soft feel** and **increased spin for control on approach shots**. TaylorMade currently starts **Tour Response** at **$42.99** and sells it around **revolutionary microcoating**, **reliable distance**, **tight left-to-right dispersion**, and a **soft** feel with **low** driver flight and **mid** wedge spin.

## Why the RXS Exists

The **Tour B RXS** matters because Bridgestone actually tells you who it is for.

Not "golfers."

Not "serious players."

Not "people who enjoy adjectives."

Golfers swinging **under 105 mph** who want:

- soft feel
- maximum distance off the tee
- increased spin for control on approach shots

That is useful.

It means the **RXS** is not just a softer premium ball. It is the **spin-first moderate-speed premium** branch in Bridgestone's lineup.

If that is exactly why you are shopping, the RXS gets very easy to defend.

## Why Tour Response Is the Smarter Default Buy

The **Tour Response** is what you buy when you want a serious urethane ball without acting like every round deserves a fifty-five-dollar dozen.

TaylorMade's current page is built around:

That is why it wins for most golfers.

## Feel: Both Are Friendly, but One Is More Purposeful

This is a softer-feel comparison, so feel alone will not decide it.

The more useful difference is what the softness is attached to.

- **RXS** attaches soft feel to a more specific moderate-speed added-spin pitch
- **Tour Response** attaches soft feel to a smarter-value urethane story

So if you are shopping only by softness, you are already leaving useful information on the table.

The right question is:

**Do I want soft feel because I want a better-value urethane ball, or do I want soft feel because I want a more specific premium fit for my speed and scoring clubs?**

Those are different golfers.
