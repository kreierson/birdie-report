The **BMW Championship** cutoff finally acted like an adult sports line instead of a decorative golf spreadsheet.

Good.

Checked on **Tuesday, August 18, 2026**, the official **PGA TOUR FedExCup** results from Memphis show:

- **50. Matt McCarty**
- **51. Keith Mitchell**
- **52. Jordan Spieth**
- **53. Max Homa**
- **54. Harris English**

That is exactly what a playoff cut is supposed to do.

It picked one guy.
It rejected several recognizable guys.
It made the difference sting.

This column is based on the official **FedExCup** results attached to the **FedEx St. Jude Championship** and checked on **Tuesday, August 18, 2026**. No pretending I built some secret better postseason in a notebook while waiting for the range to empty out.

## The Best Thing About This Cut Is That It Is Not Abstract

This is not some fake postseason where everybody gets told they are "still in a great position" while television politely avoids the consequences.

The consequences are right there:

- **McCarty** keeps playing
- **Mitchell** misses by one spot
- **Spieth** makes a run and still falls short
- **Homa** and **English** are done

That is clean.
That is legible.
That is useful.

Golf does not always explain itself well, but this part required no translator.

## Recognizable Names Missing Is the Whole Point

There is always a weird instinct in golf to treat famous names missing a line as a flaw in the format.

It is not.

It is evidence that the line had actual teeth.

If **Jordan Spieth** can spend the week clawing from **54th** and still finish **52nd**, then the number means something. If **Keith Mitchell** can arrive **49th** and leave **51st**, then every ugly swing on Sunday counted for exactly what it should count for. If **Max Homa** finishes **53rd**, nobody needs to invent some grand moral exception because the name is easier to recognize.

A playoff that never sends anyone painful home is not a playoff.
It is a marketing deck.

## Matt McCarty Is the Kind of Name the Format Should Protect

This is also why the cutoff landing on **Matt McCarty at No. 50** works.

He is not a superstar shielded by résumé.
He is not some ceremonial inclusion.
He survived the number.

That is enough.

The line should reward whoever actually stayed on the correct side of it, not whoever would look best in a sponsor graphic next week. If the format starts bending because television would rather keep a bigger name around, then the entire point of narrowing the field dies immediately.

McCarty does not need a popularity contest.
He needs the spot he earned.

## The PGA TOUR Should Stop Apologizing for This Part

We already argued in [our Wyndham piece on why the regular-season finale should feel cruel](/blog/wyndham-championship-cruel-week-opinion-2026/) and again in [the Memphis weekend column](/blog/fedex-st-jude-weekend-finally-feels-like-playoffs-opinion-2026/) that the FedExCup is at its best when the line itself creates the tension.

This is the proof.

The TOUR does not need to sell some fake version where only the trophy matters and everyone else is just background noise. The lower part of the board mattered a ton. It mattered to guys trying to reach **No. 50**, and it mattered to fans because the names around the line were real enough to feel familiar.

That is not a bug.
That is the damn product.

## My Take

The most useful thing Memphis did was not just give **Scottie Scheffler** a dominant win.

It proved the playoff cutoff can still create real discomfort without turning the whole thing into nonsense.

One spot separated **McCarty** and **Mitchell**.
Four spots separated safety from **Spieth**.
That is the sort of tension the PGA TOUR should keep leaning into instead of softening with corporate language.

Because once a playoff line starts feeling harmless, the whole format gets sleepy again.

## Bottom Line

Checked on **Tuesday, August 18, 2026**, the official **FedExCup** results from Memphis show **Matt McCarty** at **No. 50**, with **Keith Mitchell, Jordan Spieth, Max Homa,** and **Harris English** all outside the **BMW Championship** field.

That is not too harsh.
It is not bad optics.

It is exactly what a real playoff cut is supposed to do.
