The current **2026 Scotty Cameron Phantom** rollout is more useful than that. Checked on **June 17, 2026**, the official **Scotty Cameron Phantom** lineup page and current **Titleist U.S. putter listings** show a family that is broader, more segmented, and easier to shop by actual player need.

## What the Official Pages Show

As checked on June 17, 2026:

- Scotty Cameron's official Phantom page lists **11 core mallet models**
- the family includes **5, 5.2, 5.5, 5s, 7, 7.2, 7.5, 9, 9.5, 11, and 11.5**
- Scotty Cameron also highlights **OC low-torque** builds such as the **Phantom 11R OC**
- the current Titleist U.S. putter page shows standard Phantom pricing around **$499**
- the newer OC builds sit at the higher **$549** tier

That is a much clearer range map than the vague shape proliferation a lot of premium putter lines rely on.

## Why the Fit Story Matters More Than the Launch Copy

The interesting part is not just that there are more shapes. It is that the lineup is easier to sort by how a golfer actually putts.

The current official descriptions make the separation fairly clear:

- the **Phantom 5** sits in the compact, near-face-balanced wingback lane
- the **Phantom 5.2** leans into a more tour-inspired compact-mallet look
- the **Phantom 7.2** gives golfers a plumbing-neck option with more blade-like visuals in a high-MOI shape
- the **Phantom 11** is the larger, alignment-heavy, stability-first option

That is useful. It means the family is less about buying the prettiest headcover story and more about matching neck, shape, and stability profile to the stroke you actually own.

## The OC Low-Torque Expansion Is the Real Update

The part worth watching is the current **OC** push.

Scotty Cameron is now making a more explicit play into the **low-torque** conversation rather than leaving that territory entirely to other brands and other aesthetics. Golfers who want more face-stable behavior, but do not want to jump all the way into more extreme shapes, now have a more recognizable in-family option.

That does not automatically make the OC builds the best choice.

It does make the overall 2026 Phantom family more serious.

## The Smartest Models for Most Golfers

Two heads stand out as the most practical additions.

### Phantom 5.2

The **5.2** looks like the best compromise for players who want compact-mallet forgiveness without a large, busy shape. If you like cleaner visuals and a more restrained setup picture, this is the one that makes the most immediate sense.

### Phantom 7.2

The **7.2** is probably the stronger fit story. The plumbing-neck look gives golfers some blade familiarity while keeping the stability that makes modern mallets easier to live with. For golfers who want help without a spaceship behind the ball, that is a real category.

## Price Was Always Going to Be Expensive

The current listed pricing keeps the usual Scotty Cameron premium intact.

At roughly **$499** for standard Phantom models and **$549** for the OC builds on the current Titleist page, this is still luxury-putter territory. But expensive is not the same thing as confused. The more clearly this family separates stroke and visual preferences, the easier it is to justify an actual fitting conversation.
