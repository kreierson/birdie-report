Buy **Kirkland Signature** if you want the smartest receipt, the best bulk value in this comparison, and a perfectly credible urethane ball for practice rounds, casual rounds, and rounds where you know at least one tee shot is going to meet a pond.

Buy **TaylorMade Tour Response** if you want the cleaner product, the softer premium-brand feel story, and a ball that looks more like a deliberate performance choice instead of a glorious Costco loophole.

For most golfers making this exact comparison, I would recommend **Kirkland Signature**.

For the golfer who wants the more polished soft-urethane experience and is willing to pay more to get it, I would recommend **TaylorMade Tour Response**.

## Why Kirkland Wins for Most Golfers

Costco Same-Day currently shows the **Kirkland Signature 3-Piece Urethane Cover Golf Ball Performance Plus** at **$34.04** for **24 balls**.

Costco's main product page keeps the pitch simple:

- 3-piece urethane-cover golf ball
- 24-ball pack
- conforms with **USGA** and **R&A** rules
- soft and highly elastic core
- **338-dimple** pattern

That is already enough to make this comparison annoying for almost every other value-urethane ball.

Because once you can buy a real urethane-covered 24-pack at that price, the burden shifts to the other ball to justify why you should spend more.

If your first question is:

**"Which one gives me real urethane-ball credibility without making me hate the receipt?"**

the answer is still **Kirkland**.

That matters if you are the type of golfer who wants the ball to feel like an intentional part of the setup instead of just the cheapest serious option.

That is a good pitch.

TaylorMade's story feels more refined.

If you know you care about that softer branded feel and want the more polished consumer-performance product, **Tour Response** has the better case.

## Flight and Fit: Tour Response Is More Specific

TaylorMade's are not.

The current Tour Response pages explicitly frame the ball with:

- **low** driver spin
- **low** driver flight
