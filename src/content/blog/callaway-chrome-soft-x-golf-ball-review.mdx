# Callaway Chrome Soft X Golf Ball Review

Premium urethane balls are expensive. The Chrome Soft X exists so a golfer who wants a firmer, lower-spin tour ball can shop something other than a Pro V1. This review uses Callaway's and Titleist's published construction and the price gap. It does not include launch-monitor numbers, round counts, or a course diary.

## What the two balls are built to do

Callaway positions Chrome Soft X as the firmer, faster-swing sibling of Chrome Soft: a urethane cover, a firmer feel, and driver spin managed for players who do not need the softer ball's extra spin. Titleist positions the Pro V1 as the standard tour urethane ball, with short-game spin and ball-to-ball consistency as the reasons golfers pay the premium.

That is the real fork.

- **Driver.** Chrome Soft X is the lower-spin pitch. Pro V1 is the slightly higher-spin, more familiar flight. Neither difference is something this page measured in yards.
- **Irons.** Both are urethane tour balls. Expect them to be close. A fitter can separate them. A spec sheet cannot.
- **Wedges.** Pro V1's reputation is more greenside spin. Callaway still sells Chrome Soft X as a spin ball, just not as the softest one in its own family. The gap matters most if you already control partial-wedge distances.
- **Putting.** Feel is the honest difference. Chrome Soft X is the firmer click. Pro V1 is the softer one. Pick the sound you trust.

The softer Chrome Soft, not the X, is the ball for golfers who want easier compression. The X is the one Callaway aims at faster swings.

## Durability

Previous Chrome Soft generations had a reputation for scuffing easily. The cover would tear up on cart path bounces or thin wedge shots, and you'd be rotating to a new ball after 6-7 holes.

Callaway's newer Chrome Soft covers are sold as more durable than the early ones. A urethane tour ball will still scuff from a thin wedge or a cart path. That is normal. It is not a round-by-round wear test.

## The Price Factor

Here's where the Chrome Soft X makes its strongest argument.

- **Pro V1:** ~$59.99/dozen
- **Chrome Soft X:** ~$54.99/dozen

That's $5 per dozen, or about $0.42 per ball. If you play 40 rounds a year and use roughly 3 balls per round (between lost balls and wear), that's about $25 saved annually. Not life-changing, but not nothing.

Where it can add up: Chrome Soft X is discounted more often than a current Pro V1. Check the live dozen price. Do not treat an old sale as the current one.

## Ball-to-Ball Consistency

One area where Titleist keeps the reputation edge is ball-to-ball consistency. That is a brand claim golfers repeat because Titleist's tour supply chain is tight. This page did not measure an RPM gap between the two covers.

Is this a real competitive disadvantage? For most golfers, no. For a player trying to dial in exact carry distances to the yard? It's worth noting.

## Who Should Play This Ball?

If you're a mid-to-low handicap golfer with swing speed over 95 mph who wants tour-level performance and doesn't want to pay the Titleist premium, the Chrome Soft X is a fantastic choice. You're giving up very little — a touch of greenside spin, a bit of putting softness, and some ball-to-ball consistency — and saving money.

If you're a 15+ handicap, you probably don't need a $55 ball regardless. Play a Kirkland Signature or a Snell and spend the savings on lessons.

If you're a competitive scratch player where every fraction matters? The Pro V1 is still the gold standard. But the gap is much smaller than Titleist's marketing would have you believe.

## The Verdict

The Callaway Chrome Soft X is the best Pro V1 alternative on the market. It matches or comes close to the Pro V1 in every meaningful performance category, costs less, and has closed the durability gap significantly.

For most amateurs the score will not move because the logo changed. Lessons, tee shots, and short game will. The ball is the last dollar, not the first.

**Rating: 8.9/10**

Not quite a Pro V1 killer, but close enough to make you question why you're paying more.
