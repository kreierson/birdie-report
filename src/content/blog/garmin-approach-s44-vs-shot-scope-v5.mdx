The **Garmin Approach S44** is the nicer watch.

The **Shot Scope V5** is the smarter golf buy for most golfers.

That is the answer up front, and honestly it is the only reason this page exists.

The **S44** makes sense if you want Garmin's cleaner mid-price lane: brighter AMOLED screen, **43,000-plus** preloaded courses, **15 hours** of battery in GPS mode, and a more polished dedicated golf-watch experience. The **V5** makes sense if you want better golf value: full-hole maps, automatic tracking, included tags, and no subscription tax hanging around after checkout.

This is a **research-based comparison** built from Garmin's current **Approach S44** product materials, Shot Scope's current **V5** product page, and Birdie Report's existing GPS-watch cluster checked on **August 7, 2026**. No fake "I wore both for 27 rounds and entered a higher state of yardage awareness" nonsense.

## Quick Verdict

Buy the **Shot Scope V5** if you want the better golf purchase for most golfers.

Buy the **Garmin Approach S44** if you specifically want:

- the nicer screen
- the cleaner Garmin interface
- simpler glance-and-go ownership
- a watch that feels more premium on wrist even if it does less for your improvement

If I knew nothing about the buyer except "wants the smarter golf watch around three hundred bucks or less," I would point to the **Shot Scope V5** first.

If I knew the buyer hated post-round homework and mainly wanted a nicer watch to show yardages cleanly, I would point to the **S44**.

## Why the Shot Scope V5 Gets the Recommendation

The **V5** is one of the few golf-tech products whose best feature is not hidden behind another purchase.

You buy the watch and you get:

- GPS yardages
- full-hole maps
- automatic shot tracking
- included tracking tags
- post-round stats
- no subscription fees

That is a much better golf-value stack than what the **S44** offers at a higher price.

It is also why the V5 keeps making sense across the rest of the cluster, especially in [Bushnell iON Elite vs Shot Scope V5](/blog/bushnell-ion-elite-vs-shot-scope-v5/), the broader [Shot Scope V5 review](/blog/shot-scope-v5-review/), and the simpler same-budget fork in [Shot Scope V5 vs Garmin Approach S12](/blog/shot-scope-v5-vs-garmin-approach-s12/).

If you actually want your watch to help you understand where you lose shots, the **V5** is just built around a better idea.

## Where the Garmin Approach S44 Still Has a Real Case

The **S44** is not a bad value because Garmin forgot how to make watches.

It has a real appeal:

- **AMOLED** screen
- clean Garmin interface
- **43,000-plus** preloaded courses
- hazard view
- Green View
- **15-hour** GPS battery claim

That matters because some golfers do not want analytics. They do not want tags. They do not want to look at a dashboard after the round and be told their wedges are embarrassing.

They want a nicer watch that gives them the number, looks better doing it, and stops there.

That buyer absolutely exists.

And if that is you, the **S44** makes more sense than pretending you are definitely going to become a round-data guy later.
