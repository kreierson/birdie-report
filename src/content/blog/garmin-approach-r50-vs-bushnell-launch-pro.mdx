## Quick Verdict

For most premium buyers, I would still buy the **Bushnell Launch Pro** first.

If you specifically want the premium launch monitor that behaves more like a **self-contained simulator appliance**, then the **Garmin Approach R50** is the more interesting splurge.

That is the real fork.

The **Launch Pro** is the smarter buy for more golfers because it is currently **$2,000 cheaper** and still makes the stronger "I trust these numbers" case for serious indoor practice, gapping, and simulator use.

The **R50** only starts winning when the built-in **10-inch touchscreen**, on-device simulator flow, and HDMI-ready all-in-one setup matter enough to justify the extra money.

## Why the Launch Pro Still Wins for Most Buyers

Because the price gap is too big to ignore, and the cheaper product is not some compromised toy.

Bushnell's current official page still gives you a very real premium case:

- **$2,999.99** hardware
- ball data out of the box
- club data through the software path
- **Silver at $199/year**
- **Gold at $499/year**
- **GSPro** with an active Gold subscription
- **5-7 hours** of listed battery life
- **IPX7** water rating

That is already a serious spend.

But it is still a lot easier to swallow than **$4,999.99** for the R50.

And the bigger problem for Garmin is that Bushnell's cheaper box still sounds more like the tool most serious buyers actually want:

- premium indoor confidence
- fitting-bay-style data credibility
- simulator scalability
- stronger "trust the numbers" energy than mid-tier units

So this is not a case where the cheaper option is the boring compromise.

The cheaper option is the smarter premium buy.

## Where the R50 Makes a Real Case

The **Approach R50** is not just Garmin slapping a larger price tag on the R10 and calling it elite.

Garmin's current official product page and R50 explainer make a very specific argument:

- **10-inch color touchscreen**
- **three high-speed cameras**
- **impact videos**
- **HDMI output**
- **putting in the simulator**
- **43,000+ courses** through **Home Tee Hero**
- an included **carrying case**
- up to **4 hours** of battery life

That is a very different ownership mood than Bushnell.

The R50 is built for the golfer who wants the launch monitor to feel like a **complete, premium golf device**, not just a measurement engine feeding a software stack.

You can put it on the range. You can set it up indoors. You can run simulator golf without needing a separate gaming PC just to see the experience on the device itself. You can push the image outward through **HDMI** if you want the bigger theater version later.

That is the R50's whole case.

It is expensive.

But the premium appliance logic is legitimate.

## The Simulator Difference Is Bigger Than It Looks

This is the section people gloss over, and it matters.

### Garmin Approach R50

Garmin's big advantage is the built-in screen.

That means the simulator experience feels more self-contained from day one. You are not buying a premium box and then immediately feeling like you still need more hardware just to make it behave like a simulator.

Garmin's official blog also says you can use **Home Tee Hero** directly on the R50, connect through **HDMI** for a projector or monitor setup, and even **putt** in the simulator.

That is a strong pitch.

The catch is that **Home Tee Hero** still needs an active subscription.

### Bushnell Launch Pro

Bushnell's simulator story is deeper in some ways and more annoying in others.

You get the Foresight-style software ladder:

- **Silver** if you want the more restrained simulator path
- **Gold** if you want the full premium version
- **GSPro** only if you stay in the Gold lane

So Bushnell's version is less elegant but more familiar to the serious simulator crowd.

That is why the Launch Pro still feels more like the premium data platform and Garmin feels more like the premium self-contained golf-tech device.

## Why the Price Gap Is Hard to Defend for Garmin

The R50 is not competing against a $4,500 Launch Pro.

It is competing against a **$2,999.99** Launch Pro.

That changes everything.

Because once two products both live in "this is expensive enough to be irrational" territory, the one that costs **two thousand dollars less** gets a massive head start.

That extra money on the R50 is buying:

- the 10-inch built-in touchscreen
- the cleaner no-PC-needed-on-day-one simulator feel
- Garmin ecosystem preference
- the more polished all-in-one ownership story

Those are real benefits.

They are just not benefits most golfers need more than two grand.
