Sidehill putts make otherwise normal adults start seeing ghosts.

The ball is five steps from the hole, the slope is obvious, and suddenly the read turns into:

- "this has to move three cups"
- followed by a shove
- followed by a miss low because the ball was hit too hard to take any break at all

That is the sidehill-putting cycle in one dumb little scene.

Most golfers do **not** miss sidehill putts because they are incapable of seeing slope.
They miss because they combine:

- too much break in the picture
- too much speed in the stroke
- and zero discipline about where the putt should actually start and peak

So let us clean it up.

## The Rule First: Sidehill Putts Are Pace Putts Before They Are Aim Putts

If you learn one thing here, make it this:

**the faster you hit a sidehill putt, the less break you actually use.**

That sounds obvious, but golfers still read putts like they are going to die at the hole, then hit them like they are late for a flight.

For most sidehill putts, I want one of these finish windows:

- **quick or downhill sidehill**: finish **6 to 12 inches past**
- **flat to slightly uphill sidehill**: finish **12 to 18 inches past**
- **longer uphill sidehill**: finish **12 to 24 inches past**, depending on what happens after the cup

If you have not picked the finish window first, the read is fake.

If your bigger issue is seeing the line at all, start with [how to read breaking putts better](/blog/how-to-read-breaking-putts-better/) and [how to read greens like a pro](/blog/how-to-read-greens-like-a-pro/). This piece is about what to do once you already know the putt is living on a side slope.

## Use the Apex-and-6-Inch System

This is the whole model:

1. Pick the finish speed.
2. Pick the apex.
3. Pick a start spot a foot in front of the ball.
4. Roll the ball through that spot with the exact speed you chose.

That is it.

The number that keeps golfers out of trouble is the first one.

On the slippery sidehill putts that cause most three-putts, I want the ball entering with a pace that would leave the miss roughly **6 to 12 inches past**.

That smaller window matters because a pure sidehill miss does not just keep running straight by.
It usually keeps leaking with the slope and hands you a more annoying second putt than you deserved.

## The Apex Is the Part Golfers Skip

Most amateurs look at a sidehill putt like this:

- ball
- hole
- vibes

That is not a read.

I want you to identify the **apex**, meaning the highest point of the curve before the putt starts feeding toward the hole.

General sidehill defaults:

- **gentle sidehill**: apex around **60%** of the way to the hole
- **faster green**: apex closer to **50% to 55%**
- **slight uphill with side slope**: apex can move a touch later because the uphill portion fights some of the sideways movement

Those are not sacred formulas.
They are checkpoints that stop you from pretending the whole break happens in the last two feet.

Because it usually does not.

## The Start Spot Should Be 12 to 18 Inches in Front of the Ball

Do not aim at "high side."
That is lazy golfer language.

Pick something specific:

- an old ball mark
- a dark blade patch
- a scuff
- or literally a point on the grass **12 to 18 inches** in front of the ball

Why so close?

Because that is the only part of the putt you directly control.

You do not roll the ball at the cup first.
You roll it through the first foot first.

If your start line has been ugly lately, pair this with [lag putting tips to eliminate three-putts](/blog/lag-putting-tips-eliminate-three-putts/). Better long-range pace makes the sidehill reads feel much less dramatic.

## My On-Course Read in 15 Seconds

This is the actual routine:

1. Decide whether the putt is pure sidehill, sidehill-uphill, or sidehill-downhill.
2. Pick the finish window first.
3. Stand halfway to the hole and identify the apex.
4. Walk back and pick a start spot **12 to 18 inches** in front of the ball.
5. Commit to one speed and roll it.

If you are still hovering over the putt after that, you are usually not gathering information.
You are manufacturing doubt.

## Three Sidehill Putt Types and the Right Speed

### 1. Pure sidehill, medium speed green

This is the standard club-championship troublemaker.

Plan:

- read enough break to let the ball work down naturally
- choose a miss that finishes **8 to 12 inches past**
- and do not turn it into a hit

### 2. Sidehill and downhill

This is the one that ruins scorecards.

Plan:

- tighten the pace window to **6 to 8 inches past**
- expect the ball to start yielding earlier
- and accept that dying it near the high edge is better than ramming it low and three-putting

If these are your nightmare fuel, also read [how to putt downhill without three-putting](/blog/how-to-putt-downhill-without-three-putting/). The pace window is almost always the real argument there too.

### 3. Sidehill and uphill

This is the most makeable version if you stay sane.

Plan:

- allow a slightly firmer roll
- keep the finish window around **12 to 18 inches past**
- and resist the urge to under-read it just because the putt is climbing

That is the trap.

Golfers think "uphill means straighter," then never give the side slope enough respect.

If uphill pace is your bigger issue, go read [how to putt uphill without leaving it short](/blog/how-to-putt-uphill-without-leaving-it-short/). Use both systems together instead of inventing a new stroke every time the green tilts a little differently.

## The Two Misses That Tell You What Went Wrong

### Miss high and short

That usually means one of two things:

- you over-read the putt
- or you hit the pace you wanted, but the pace you wanted was too dying

### Miss low and long

That is the classic amateur sidehill miss.

It usually means:

- you hit it too hard
- which turned a breaking putt into a straight-ish putt
- and then the ball kept running because the slope still existed after the hole

That is why "I under-read it" is often the wrong diagnosis.

A lot of the time you did not under-read it.
You **over-hit it**.

## Drill 1: The 10-Ball Capture-Speed Ladder

This is the fastest way to fix speed discipline.

### Setup

- Find a sidehill putt from **15 to 20 feet**
- Put a tee **8 inches** past the hole
- Put another tee **18 inches** past the hole

### Rules

- Hit **10 balls**
- Every ball has to finish between those tees if it misses
- Goal: **7 of 10**

You are teaching yourself that a sidehill putt is not a free-for-all.

## Drill 2: The Apex Tee Drill

This one cleans up reads fast.

### Setup

- Find a sidehill putt from **10 to 15 feet**
- Pick what you believe is the apex
- Drop a tee there

### Rules

- Hit **8 putts**
- Track how many start online and curve near the tee you picked
- If most balls stay below it, you under-read
- If most balls finish above it and die, you probably over-read or chose too-dead speed

Goal: get **5 of 8** to travel through the correct curve window.

## Drill 3: The One-Handicap Checkpoint Game

This is the on-course version.

From inside **20 feet**, every time you face a real sidehill putt ask yourself three questions:

1. What is my finish window?
2. Where is the apex?
3. What is the exact start spot?

If you cannot answer those in under **10 seconds**, you do not have a read yet.

## What Good Sidehill Putting Actually Looks Like

It does **not** look like:

- always dying the ball at the lip
- always playing a mile of break
- or trying to "hold the face off" through impact like you are landing a plane

It looks like:

- a specific start line
- a predictable pace window
- and boring second putts when you miss

That last part matters.

You do not need every sidehill putt to threaten the center of the cup.
You need the misses to stop turning into preventable three-putts.

## Bottom Line

If you want to putt sidehill putts better, stop treating break as the only story.

Use the **apex-and-6-inch pace system**:

- pick the finish speed first
- identify the apex
- choose a real start spot
- and make the slope earn the break at the pace you planned

That is how you stop overplaying break, stop blasting it low, and start leaving yourself the kind of second putts that do not make you question your entire existence.
