The useful 2026 driver question is no longer "which 2023 head still looks fast in a roundup." Titleist, TaylorMade, and Callaway have all moved to a newer family. PING's G440 family is still the current PING driver line. This guide starts there.

It is a research roundup, not a personal test. The model names, fit stories, and prices below come from manufacturer pages and from Birdie Report articles that already cite those pages. Nothing here is a carry number, a dispersion number, or a score from a round this site played.

## Where to start

**Best current forgiveness start:** [PING G440 Max](/blog/ping-g440-max-driver-review/) — the broad-fit head in PING's current driver family  
**Best current Titleist:** [Titleist GTS2](/blog/titleist-gts2-driver-review/) — Titleist's broad-fit GTS head, in shops since June 11, 2026  
**Best current TaylorMade:** Qi4D — the 2026 flagship family, listed at $649.99 on TaylorMade's product page  
**Best current Callaway:** [Quantum Max](/blog/callaway-quantum-max-driver-review/) — Callaway's 2026 total-performance head. The January 16, 2026 announcement started drivers at $649.99  
**Best value, previous generation:** Titleist GT2, TaylorMade Qi35, or Callaway Elyte, only when the live price is clearly under the current head

A fitting still matters more than this list. Shaft, length, and loft change the result more than the logo on the crown.

## PING G440 Max: the current forgiveness start

PING describes the G440 Max as the most forgiving model in that launch family and the one that fits most golfers. The January 14, 2025 release lists a 460cc head, a 29-gram three-position back weight, and Trajectory Tuning 2.0 with ±1.5° of loft adjustment. Standard length is 46 inches in the Alta CB Blue 50 and 45.5 inches in several other stock shafts.

That is the reason it leads this list for golfers whose contact moves around the face. It is not a claim that it is longer than every other current head on a center strike.

The same family has other jobs:

- **G440 LST** is the lower-spin, 450cc head for faster swings. The [LST review](/blog/ping-g440-lst-driver-review/) is the page for that fit.
- **G440 SFT** is the slice-correction head.
- **G440 K** is the later high-MOI head. PING's driver page calls it the highest total MOI in the family so far. This site does not have a separate K review, so start with the Max review and let a fitter move you if the K is the better match.

The full research write-up is the [PING G440 Max driver review](/blog/ping-g440-max-driver-review/). Check the current price there or on PING's site. This page is not pinning one.

## Titleist GTS2: the current Titleist head

Titleist's May 13, 2026 release split the new family into three jobs. GTS2 is speed with maximum stability and a high-launch, mid-spin profile. GTS3 is the more adjustable, lower-launch head. GTS4 is the lowest-spin model, now in a 460cc head. Standard MSRP was $699.

GTS2 is the Titleist head to put on a fitting card for most golfers. GTS3 is the one to add if you want the track-weight adjustability and a more compact profile. The [GTS2 review](/blog/titleist-gts2-driver-review/) and the [official launch story](/blog/titleist-gts-drivers-official-launch-2026/) have the longer version.

GT2 and GT3 are not the current Titleist drivers. They belong in the value section below.

## TaylorMade Qi4D: the current TaylorMade head

TaylorMade's current driver page is the Qi4D family: Qi4D, Qi4D LS, Qi4D Max, and Qi4D Max Lite, each listed at $649.99 when checked on November 4, 2026. The standard Qi4D page describes a reshaped head, a carbon face, and four movable Trajectory Adjustment System weights, two at 9 grams and two at 4 grams.

This site does not have a standalone Qi4D review. The recommendation here is the manufacturer's published family, not a measured ball-speed gap versus Qi35. Read [why TaylorMade is keeping Qi4D through a two-year cycle](/blog/taylormade-two-year-driver-cycle-opinion-2026/) before you treat a 2025 Qi35 as the current TaylorMade driver.

Use the standard Qi4D as the all-around starting point, the Max when forgiveness is the whole point, the LS when you already fight spin, and the Max Lite when the build needs to be lighter. Confirm the exact head on the product page. The names are not interchangeable.

## Callaway Quantum Max: the current Callaway head

Callaway's January 16, 2026 announcement made Quantum the new driver family: Max, Max Fast, Max D, Triple Diamond, and Triple Diamond Max, at retail from February 13, with drivers starting at $649.99. The Quantum Max page describes a Tri-Force Face — titanium, a polymer mesh, and carbon fiber — plus a 10-gram weight with neutral and draw settings and an OptiFit hosel.

Callaway's own comparison on that family page says Elyte was the prior generation. Quantum Max is the total-performance starting point. Max D is the draw-biased head. Max Fast is the lighter build for moderate swing speeds. Triple Diamond is the more compact, lower-spin option.

The research write-up is the [Callaway Quantum Max driver review](/blog/callaway-quantum-max-driver-review/), including the November 5, 2026 price check. The [Elyte review](/blog/callaway-elyte-driver-review/) is about the previous family.

## Best value: previous generation

Buy a previous-generation head when the live price is the reason, not because a roundup still leads with it.

**Titleist GT2.** Titleist lists it as a 2024–2025 model. It remains a real fitting option if you want SureFit and the price is under a GTS2. Start with the [GT2 review](/blog/titleist-gt2-driver-review/) and [GT2 vs GTS2](/blog/titleist-gt2-vs-titleist-gts2-driver/).

**TaylorMade Qi35.** This was the flagship before Qi4D. The [Qi35 review](/blog/taylormade-qi35-driver-review/) is the right page when a retailer is clearing that family. Do not pay current-flagship money for it.

**Callaway Elyte.** Callaway's [Elyte driver page](https://www.callawaygolf.com/golf-clubs/drivers/drivers-2025-elyte.html) still sells the 2025 head. On November 4, 2026 it listed $399.99, reduced from $499.99. That is the value case: a prior-generation Callaway at a clear discount, not a substitute for reading the Quantum spec if you want the current head. The [Elyte review](/blog/callaway-elyte-driver-review/) covers the older tech story.

**Older than that.** TaylorMade Stealth 2 Plus, Callaway Paradym X, Cobra LTDx, and Titleist TSR3 are another cycle back. They only make sense if the specific club in front of you is priced below a GT2, Qi35, or Elyte you can actually buy, and the shaft still fits. This page is not treating them as 2026 primary picks, and it does not have a verified current price for them.

Cobra's Darkspeed Max and DS-ADAPT reviews on this site are previous-cycle coverage. The [DS-ADAPT MAX-K review](/blog/cobra-ds-adapt-max-k-driver-review/) already notes that OPTM is the newer Cobra family.

## What to ignore in a driver ad

Shaft and delivery beat the head name. A current head in the wrong shaft is a worse purchase than a previous-generation head that a fitter already matched to your swing.

Ask the fitter for the miss pattern, not the longest single drive. Ten swings with the bad ones left in the average tell you more than a robot number on a product page.

Do not pay a new-family price for a head from the cycle before last just because the badge is familiar. And do not skip a discounted GT2, Qi35, or Elyte when the current head is $650 to $699 and the older one is the club you actually hit.
