## Quick Verdict

At the same current price, I would buy the **Garmin Approach R50** first for most golfers.

I would only buy the **Full Swing KIT** first if I knew the golfer specifically wanted:

- premium **radar**
- no-unlock core data
- stronger range / outdoor flexibility
- the cleaner "no big simulator membership speech on day one" ownership mood

That is the whole page.

At **$4,999** either way, the R50 gives you the more obvious premium thing you can actually feel every session: the built-in **10-inch touchscreen**, the more self-contained simulator flow, and the cleaner "this is the device" experience.

The KIT still has a very real case, but it is the more specific case.

## Why the R50 Wins at Equal MSRP

Because when two launch monitors cost five grand, the one with the stronger built-in experience should usually win.

Garmin's current official pitch gives you:

- a **10-inch color touchscreen**
- **three high-speed cameras**
- **impact videos**
- **HDMI output**
- simulator golf on **43,000+ courses** through **Home Tee Hero**
- the ability to **putt** in the simulator
- an included **carrying case**

That is a lot of tangible premium value.

The R50 feels like the product a golfer buys when they want the launch monitor to behave like a **complete premium golf machine**, not just a very expensive measurement box.

At the exact same MSRP as the KIT, that is hard to ignore.

Yes, Garmin still wants an active subscription for **Home Tee Hero**.

Yes, that is annoying.

But even with that caveat, the R50 still gives you the more obvious day-to-day premium payoff for the same money.

## Why the Full Swing KIT Still Has a Legitimate Case

The **KIT** is still a very good launch monitor.

Its case is just narrower.

Full Swing's official page still makes four strong arguments:

- **16 club and ball data points** with **no unlock**
- **free cloud-based app**
- indoor and outdoor use
- **30-day money-back guarantee**

That matters because a lot of premium launch-monitor ownership gets annoying fast.

The KIT is trying to avoid that mood.

It wants to be the premium-radar product where you buy the hardware, get the data, use the app, and stop feeling like the company is waiting to invoice you again every time you get excited.

That is real value.

It is just not as immediately visible as Garmin's giant built-in screen.

## This Is Really Touchscreen Sim Convenience vs Radar Ownership Cleanliness

That is the cleanest way to frame it.

### Garmin Approach R50

You are paying for:

- built-in simulator feel
- camera-based precision story
- HDMI-ready flexibility
- less dependence on extra gear just to enjoy the premium part of the product

### Full Swing KIT

You are paying for:

- premium radar
- included core data without an unlock
- easier emotional ownership than Bushnell-style subscriptions
- a stronger "take it to the range and trust the radar workflow" personality

That is why both products can make sense without being the same recommendation.

## The Software Story Is Messy in Different Ways

Nobody in this category knows how to just sell a box and shut up.

### Garmin R50

Garmin's current product page makes it clear that **Home Tee Hero** requires an active subscription.

So while the R50 looks like the cleaner standalone simulator, the best simulator part of the ownership story still wants a membership.

### Full Swing KIT

Full Swing's base ownership story is cleaner:

- free app
- included data
- no unlock for the core 16 data points

But once you start caring about richer virtual play, Full Swing still points you toward separately purchased software paths or its broader **KIT Studio** ecosystem.

So the KIT is cleaner than some premium rivals.

It is not magically free of simulator-spending gravity.

## Indoor and Outdoor Use: The KIT's Best Counterpunch

This is where the **KIT** stays dangerous.

Full Swing's official page currently says:

- place the unit about **10 feet behind the ball outdoors**
- allow **8-10 feet behind the ball indoors**
- allow **8-10 feet of ball flight** indoors

That still reads like a serious radar unit meant for golfers who really do want the launch monitor to move between:

- range sessions
- indoor practice
- backyard work
- premium-home setups

The **R50** can move too.

But Garmin's premium pitch still feels more **sim-first** and **screen-first**, while Full Swing feels more **radar-first** and **practice-everywhere-first**.

If that difference matters to you, it matters a lot.
