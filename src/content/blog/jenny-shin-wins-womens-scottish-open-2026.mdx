**Jenny Shin** made this way harder than she probably wanted.

She still won it anyway.

According to the **LPGA's official results page** and an **Associated Press report published Sunday, July 26, 2026**, checked on **Monday, July 27**, Shin won the **Women's Scottish Open** at **9-under par**, two ahead of **A Lim Kim**, for her **first LPGA victory since the 2016 Volunteers of America Texas Shootout**.

That is a hell of a way to end a 10-year wait.

This piece is based on the **LPGA's official tournament results page**, the **LPGA/AP story published July 26, 2026**, and an additional **AP syndication report from Sunday**, all checked on **Monday, July 27, 2026**. No pretending I was tucked behind a Scottish dune tracking nerves in real time.

For the week as it built, read [our first-round recap on Shin and Lauren Coughlin sharing the lead](/blog/womens-scottish-open-round-1-shin-coughlin-2026/), [our halfway story after Shin opened a five-shot gap](/blog/jenny-shin-five-shot-womens-scottish-open-lead-2026/), [our Sunday opinion on why the 10-year wait mattered](/blog/jenny-shin-ten-year-wait-opinion-2026/), and [the broader column on why this event should stop being framed like a major warm-up](/blog/womens-scottish-open-not-a-warm-up-opinion-2026/).

## Shin Had Plenty of Chances To Let This Get Weird

The AP report says Shin shot a **3-over 75** in the final round after starting Sunday with a **five-shot lead**.

That is the part that makes the win feel heavier.

She bogeyed **three straight holes from the sixth through the eighth**, according to AP, and later dropped **two of her last three holes** as the tournament started to wobble a little. That is the kind of round that can go from "comfortable lead" to "what the hell just happened" in a hurry.

It just never fully flipped.

Shin had already done enough over the first three days to keep the whole thing upright.

## The Leaderboard Finished Exactly Tight Enough

The **LPGA's official results page**, checked **Monday, July 27**, lists:

- **Jenny Shin** at **-9**
- **A Lim Kim** at **-7**
- **Pajaree Anannarukarn** at **-5**
- **Esther Henseleit** at **-4**
- **Lauren Coughlin** and **Miyu Yamashita** tied at **-3**

That is a much better tournament shape than a Sunday cruise.

AP says **A Lim Kim** closed with a **4-under 68**, using **four straight birdies** to leap into solo second. **Anannarukarn**, who began the day as Shin's closest pursuer, slid back with a **74** and finished third.

So yes, Shin had room.
But she also still had people behind her doing enough to make the finish serious.

## Shin Sounded Like This One Counted More

The AP report quotes Shin saying her first LPGA win in **2016** did not fully feel deserved and felt "very accidental," while this one felt earned.

That is the exact right quote for a win like this.

Because this was not some random Thursday-Friday heater that held together just long enough. This was a wire-to-wire week, on a real Scottish test, with enough late mistakes to make the player actually prove she could absorb the moment instead of just float through it.

That is a sturdier kind of victory.

## Dundonald Gave the Story Some Edge

One of the more useful things about this result is where it happened.

**Dundonald Links** is not some decorative stop where a veteran can sleepwalk to a feel-good trophy. The course asks for actual control, and the final-round wobble almost made the whole thing much nastier.

That is part of why the win lands so well.

It was not clean.
It was not stress-free.
It was still real.

## The Timing Before Royal Lytham Matters Too

AP notes the **Women's Scottish Open** sits between the **Evian Championship** and the **AIG Women's Open**, which begins **Thursday, July 30, 2026** at **Royal Lytham & St. Annes**.

That means Shin did not just win a nice standalone event.

She won the week that golf people keep lazily describing as a bridge, and she did it in a way that should make that framing look dumb again. This tournament already had a legit field, a legit course, and a legit story. Sunday just finished the proof.

## Bottom Line

**Jenny Shin** won the **2026 Women's Scottish Open** on **Sunday, July 26**, according to the **LPGA's official results** and the **AP's final report** checked **Monday, July 27**, finishing at **9-under** and beating **A Lim Kim** by **two shots** for her first LPGA title since **2016**.

It was messy late.
It was still deserved.

And it was the exact kind of veteran win that makes this part of the women's golf calendar worth taking seriously on its own.
