The easiest lazy take in golf is still:

post-major week, weak field, wake me up later.

Then somebody does something impossible to ignore and everybody has to act surprised that the **PGA TOUR** season did not actually stop existing after **The Open Championship**.

That is what **Michael Kim's 59** did Friday.

According to the **Associated Press round-two report published Friday, July 24, 2026**, Kim had even considered **skipping** the **3M Open** after five straight weeks on the road. Then he showed up at **TPC Twin Cities**, shot a **bogey-free 59**, made a **24-foot birdie putt on the 18th**, and took a **three-shot lead through 36 holes**.

That is the exact kind of thing filler weeks are not supposed to produce.

This column is based on the **AP's July 24, 2026 round-two report**, as published by the **PGA TOUR** and other syndication outlets, checked on **Saturday, July 25, 2026**. For the straight-news version first, read [our Michael Kim round-two recap](/blog/michael-kim-59-3m-open-2026/). For the broader tournament context, read [why the 3M Open is a career-pressure week](/blog/3m-open-career-week-opinion-2026/) and [our Thursday take on Jackson Koivun's 64](/blog/jackson-koivun-3m-open-64-opinion-2026/).

## This Is the Part of the Season Where Weird Things Are Supposed to Happen

That is not an insult.

It is the point.

Late July on the TOUR should not be a polished waiting room. It should be unstable. It should be where:

- players who missed the cut at a major come back irritated
- bubble guys remember their season is still on the line
- younger names try to force themselves into the conversation
- and somebody who nearly took the week off accidentally detonates the whole tournament

That last one is Kim.

The AP report says he had missed cuts at **The Open**, the **Genesis Scottish Open**, and the **John Deere Classic**, and that his best finish since his **Valero Texas Open runner-up** was only **17th**. That is not the résumé of a player walking into Minnesota with headline energy.

That is exactly why the round mattered.

## A 59 Still Smashes Through Every Fake Calendar Hierarchy

Golf loves over-organizing itself.

Major weeks.
Signature weeks.
Playoff weeks.
Weeks we are told count.
Weeks we are told to treat like decorative bridge material.

Then somebody shoots **59** and all that tidy hierarchy looks pretty stupid.

Because the sport still works best when the golf itself can interrupt the calendar.

It does not matter that this was not Augusta.
It does not matter that it was not a playoff event.
It does not matter that half the discourse machine wanted to keep talking about who looked tired after Royal Birkdale.

Kim made the number too big to ignore.

That is one of the healthiest things a long season can still do.

## The Best Story Was Not That Scottie Was There

This is another annoying reflex golf should quit.

Whenever something wild happens in a non-major, the first instinct is to drag the nearest superstar back into the middle of it and ask what the result means for him.

No.

The AP recap says **Scottie Scheffler** was **seven back** after a **70** with a double bogey at **14** and a bogey at **17**. Fine. That keeps him alive for the weekend.

But Friday's value was not that the world No. 1 was nearby while history happened. Friday's value was that somebody else got to own the day completely.

That is how tour events outside the biggest weeks keep their pulse.

## Kim Is a Better Version of the "Why We Still Watch" Argument

We already made the structural case in [our 3M Open column](/blog/3m-open-career-week-opinion-2026/) and the next-name case in [our Koivun piece](/blog/jackson-koivun-3m-open-64-opinion-2026/).

Kim adds a third version:

the redemption-and-whiplash case.

He is not some random stranger popping out of nowhere. He is also not one of the untouchable names the sport markets by default. He lives in the middle, which is where most weekly golf actually happens:

- good enough to matter
- flawed enough to wobble
- close enough to his past form to be interesting
- far enough from it lately that one round can reset the whole mood

That is why the 59 hit so well.

It was not just history for history's sake. It was history arriving through a player whose season had gotten messy enough to feel human again.

## This Is Also Why a Long Season Should Stay a Long Season

Golf keeps flirting with the idea that what it really needs is fewer meaningful weeks and more concentrated star power.

I think that is backward.

You need the stars, sure. But you also need room for the schedule to breathe and turn sideways. You need tournaments where a guy can go from nearly skipping the event to standing on the last green with a putt for **59** and a weekend lead.

That is not clutter.
That is narrative oxygen.

A sport that only matters on pre-approved weeks stops feeling like a season and starts feeling like a content package.

Kim's Friday was the opposite of that.

Good.

## Bottom Line

**Michael Kim's bogey-free 59** at the **3M Open** on **Friday, July 24, 2026** is the best current argument against calling late-July PGA TOUR golf filler.

It came from a player who had missed his last three cuts, briefly considered skipping the week, and then took a **three-shot lead through 36 holes**.

That is not dead time.

That is exactly what a real season is supposed to leave room for.
