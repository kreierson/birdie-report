The laziest way to sell this week's **AIG Women's Open** is to turn it into one-player content and call it clean television.

That would also miss the point.

The current **LPGA Race to CME Globe** page, checked on **Tuesday, July 28, 2026**, lists:

- **Nelly Korda** first at **3,412.625**
- **Haeran Ryu** second at **2,252.798**
- **Hyo Joo Kim** third at **1,786.419**
- **Miyu Yamashita** fourth at **1,771.543**
- **Jeeno Thitikul** fifth at **1,546.625**
- **Hannah Green** sixth at **1,352.200**
- **Lottie Woad** seventh at **1,333.964**

That is not one main character and six background extras.

That is a final major with an actual cast.

## Korda Is the Headliner, Not the Entire Plot

This is not an anti-**Nelly Korda** argument.

She is still the biggest name in the field and the current **Race to CME Globe** leader by a pretty healthy margin. That part is real. We have already spent plenty of time on [Korda's Hall of Fame chase](/blog/nelly-korda-hall-of-fame-chase-opinion-2026/) and [what her U.S. Women's Open win did to the season](/blog/nelly-korda-us-womens-open-changes-lpga-season-2026/).

But the mistake is pretending that because she is the cleanest lead story, she must also be the only story worth building around.

That is weak packaging, not strong storytelling.

## The Field Depth Is the Selling Point

Look at the current board again.

**Haeran Ryu** already has a major and another major-adjacent summer run behind her. **Miyu Yamashita** is the defending champion. **Jeeno Thitikul** keeps showing up near the top of the season's meaningful conversations. **Lottie Woad** is already far enough up the current points list that nobody should be using "nice young player" language anymore.

That is before you even get to the names the **LPGA media center** has slotted into pre-tournament availability on **July 28** and **July 29**:

- **Lottie Woad**
- **Jeeno Thitikul**
- **Charley Hull**
- **Haeran Ryu**
- **Miyu Yamashita**
- **Lydia Ko**
- **Georgia Hall**
- **Nelly Korda**

That is a week begging for breadth, not simplification.

## Women's Golf Keeps Getting Undersold by Over-Tidying the Narrative

This has been a recurring problem.

Women's golf finally gets a major with a **record $10 million purse**, a recognizable venue, and a strong set of current contenders, and the first instinct is still to ask which one person should carry the poster.

Why?

Men's golf gets treated like a full ecosystem all the time. Multiple contenders, multiple subplots, multiple carryover arcs. Nobody acts like a major is broken unless one face dominates every promo.

Women's golf deserves the same confidence.

We already made a version of this point in [our LPGA growth piece](/blog/lpga-growth-finally-looks-structural-2026/), in [the Lottie Woad column](/blog/lottie-woad-second-lpga-win-opinion-2026/), and in [our take on why the Women's Scottish Open should not be treated like pre-major wallpaper](/blog/womens-scottish-open-not-a-warm-up-opinion-2026/).

This is the same argument with a bigger stage.

## Royal Lytham Should Feel Crowded With Possibility

The **R&A's current championship page** says this is the **50th staging** of the **AIG Women's Open**, with **144 players** at **Royal Lytham & St Annes** and **Miyu Yamashita** returning as defending champion.

That should feel busy.
It should feel layered.
It should feel like more than one possible ending makes sense.

That is not a problem to solve away.
That is the actual appeal.

## My Take

The strongest version of this major is not "Can anybody stop Nelly?"

The strongest version is:

- can **Korda** keep control of the season
- can **Ryu** keep escalating
- can **Yamashita** defend
- can **Jeeno** turn another big week into something heavier
- can **Woad** force the conversation into a new category again

That is better television.
It is better writing.
It is also just more honest.

## Bottom Line

The official **LPGA standings checked on Tuesday, July 28, 2026** show **Nelly Korda** leading the **Race to CME Globe**, but they also show a top end stacked with **Haeran Ryu**, **Hyo Joo Kim**, **Miyu Yamashita**, **Jeeno Thitikul**, **Hannah Green**, and **Lottie Woad**.

This **AIG Women's Open** does not need one designated main character.

It needs golf to stop pretending depth is a marketing problem.
