A lot of golfers use the tee box on par 3s like it is a permission slip to get dumb.

They peg the ball up too high, make the same swing they use with driver in their imagination, and then act surprised when the iron shot comes out:

- thin
- floaty
- too spinny
- or weirdly short for how "good" it felt

That is not mysterious.

That is a bad tee height turning an iron shot into a fake wood shot.

If you want the bigger hole-management picture first, read [how to play par 3s better](/blog/how-to-play-par-3s-strategy-guide/), [how to play front pins without making bogey](/blog/how-to-play-front-pins-without-making-bogey/), and [how to play middle pins better](/blog/how-to-play-middle-pins-better/). This page is the narrower fix for the one small setup mistake that keeps screwing up otherwise normal iron swings.

## The Job of the Tee on a Par 3

The tee is not there to launch the ball higher.

The tee is there to give you:

- a perfectly clean lie
- no grass trapped behind the ball
- and one less excuse for bad contact

That is it.

With an iron, you still want the club to behave like an iron:

- ball first
- then turf
- with a normal, boring descending strike

If you tee it too high, you start changing the picture enough that your brain wants to help the ball up. That is where the strike gets goofy.

## The Low-Tee Rule

For most iron shots on par 3s, the ball should sit only **barely above the turf**.

My default guide:

- **4- to 6-iron:** about **1/8 inch** above the ground
- **7- to 9-iron:** about **1/16 to 1/8 inch**
- **PW or less:** basically **flush to barely proud** of the turf

If you need a visual:

- with a **mid iron**, I want the ball to look like it is sitting on a tiny pedestal, not a launch pad
- with a **short iron**, I want it to look almost like a perfect fairway lie with training wheels

If you can see a bunch of daylight under the ball with an 8-iron, you probably went too high.

## Why Higher Is Usually Worse

Too-high iron tees create three problems fast.

### 1. You start chasing lift

Once the ball looks elevated, golfers instinctively try to sweep it.

Now the swing gets:

- shallower than it should
- more flippy
- and less committed through the ground

That is a nice recipe for thin missiles and pop-up nonsense.

### 2. You move strike up the face

A slightly high-face strike with an iron can feel clean and still cost you:

- ball speed
- distance control
- and predictable launch

On a par 3, that usually means coming up short while acting offended about it.

### 3. You start making a different swing than your stock swing

This is the big one.

The whole point of teeing an iron on a par 3 is to let you make your normal iron motion from a cleaner lie.

If the tee height makes you change the motion, the tee stopped helping.

## My Default Setup on Iron Par 3s

Once the tee height is right, the rest should feel boring:

- weight **55 percent** on the lead side
- ball position **normal for that club**, not shoved forward because it is on a tee
- chest and sternum staying over the ball
- finish fully through the shot, not hanging back to watch it

That last part matters.

Golfers tee an iron a little high, then hang back because the picture tells them the ball should be "picked."

Nope.

Still hit the iron shot.

If your basic contact is already shaky, pair this with [how to strike your irons pure](/blog/how-to-strike-irons-pure-ball-first-contact/) before you go hunting for more exotic fixes.

## The Club-by-Club Check I Use

### Long iron par 3s

For **4-, 5-, or 6-iron**:

- tee it about **1/8 inch**
- keep the same normal ball position
- and make the same through-the-ball strike you would use from a perfect fairway lie

I want help from the clean lie, not help from a lifted launch.

If the hole is long and windy, go reread [how to play headwind approach shots without ballooning it](/blog/how-to-play-headwind-approach-shots-without-ballooning-it/) and [how to hit more greens from 175-200 yards](/blog/approach-strategy-175-200-yards/). The answer is usually better club math, not more tee height.

### Mid iron par 3s

For **7-, 8-, or 9-iron**:

- **1/16 to 1/8 inch** is plenty
- the ball should look only slightly elevated
- and the strike should still feel down-and-through

This is the scoring zone where golfers talk themselves into high tee nonsense because the hole looks attackable.

Do not turn a good yardage into a launch experiment.

### Short iron or wedge par 3s

For **PW, gap wedge, sand wedge**:

- tee nearly flush
- or push the tee down so the ball only sits just proud of the turf

These clubs already have loft.

They do not need extra help.

They need a clean strike and a flight you can actually control.

## The 12-Ball Tee-Height Drill

This is the fastest way I know to stop guessing.

Use **three clubs**:

- **6-iron**
- **8-iron**
- **pitching wedge**

Hit **4 balls with each club**.

### 6-iron station

Hit:

- **2 balls** teed at **1/8 inch**
- **2 balls** teed at about **1/4 inch**

What you are checking:

- which height gives you the tighter start line
- which height gives you the flatter, more stable flight
- which height produces the more centered strike

For most golfers, the **1/8-inch** version wins immediately.

### 8-iron station

Hit:

- **2 balls** teed at **1/16 inch**
- **2 balls** teed at about **3/16 inch**

Goal:

- at least **3 of 4** should start on line and hold their normal window

If the higher tee starts producing floaty, high-spin garbage, congratulations, you found the problem.

### Pitching-wedge station

Hit:

- **2 balls** nearly flush
- **2 balls** at **1/8 inch**

Goal:

- all **4** should come out with a predictable yardage window
- at least **3 of 4** should finish in your normal distance gap, not balloon short

Score the drill like this:

- **2 points** for centered strike plus normal flight window
- **1 point** for playable contact with slightly wrong flight
- **0 points** for thin, floaty, or obvious high-face nonsense

Maximum score: **24**

If your lower tee setup outscores the higher one by **4 or more points**, stop debating and start using the lower tee on the course.

## The Three On-Course Checkpoints

Before any iron par 3, ask:

### 1. Does the ball look like a clean fairway lie or a little mini-driver?

If it looks like a mini-driver, tee it down.

### 2. Am I trying to help the shot up?

If yes, the setup picture is already wrong.

Tee it lower, take the correct club, and make the same swing you trust from the fairway.

### 3. Is wind making me want more height when I actually need less?

This is common into a breeze.

Golfers see a long par 3, panic, tee it higher, and hit a balloon.

That is backwards.

Into the wind, I want:

- lower tee
- more club
- calmer swing

Not more launch cosplay.

## Common Mistakes

### Teeing every iron the same height

A 5-iron and a wedge should not sit the same.

Longer iron, slightly more tee.
Shorter iron, almost nothing.

### Shoving the ball forward because it is on a tee

The tee does not change the club's job.

Keep the ball position normal for the iron.

### Judging the shot only by height

Higher does not mean better.

I care about:

- centered strike
- normal carry
- stable start line

If the shot comes out pretty but flies short, you did not win anything.

## What I Want You To See in the Ball Flight

A good iron par-3 shot should look:

- slightly cleaner than a fairway shot
- not dramatically higher
- and very normal through the middle of the flight

If it looks like it is climbing forever, that is usually not extra "hold the green" magic.

That is usually a strike or launch problem dressed up as optimism.

## Bottom Line

On par 3s, the tee should make the lie cleaner, not the swing different.

Use this default:

- **4- to 6-iron:** about **1/8 inch**
- **7- to 9-iron:** **1/16 to 1/8 inch**
- **PW and wedges:** almost flush

Then run the **12-ball tee-height drill** and keep the version that gives you the tighter flight and cleaner strike.

That is how the tee box becomes an advantage instead of a stupid little sabotage device.
