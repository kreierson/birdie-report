The **2026 AIG Women's Open** got the exact kind of ending a proper major should get:

some nerves, some chaos, one brutally good putt to force extra holes, and a winner almost nobody had penciled in as the main character four days earlier.

According to the **LPGA's official winner story published Sunday, August 2, 2026**, plus the **AIG Women's Open's official recap** and **Reuters reporting from Sunday**, **Shiho Kuwaki** beat **Esther Henseleit** in a **two-hole playoff** to win the **50th AIG Women's Open** at **Royal Lytham & St Annes**. The official championship recap says Kuwaki finished regulation at **5-under**, only for Henseleit to hole a huge birdie putt at the last to force the playoff.

This piece is based on the **LPGA's August 2 winner report**, the **AIG Women's Open's official Sunday recap**, **Reuters' Sunday final report**, and the current championship results checked on **Monday, August 3, 2026**. No pretending I was inside the ropes at Royal Lytham collecting playoff vibes for the people.

For the runway into this finish, start with [our field breakdown for the 50th edition](/blog/aig-womens-open-field-50th-edition-2026/), [the Annika award-race setup](/blog/aig-womens-open-annika-award-race-2026/), and [our earlier take on why this major did not need one star to carry it](/blog/aig-womens-open-no-single-main-character-opinion-2026/).

## Henseleit Forced the Extra Holes, Then Kuwaki Finished the Job

The cleanest detail from the official recaps is that **Kuwaki had already posted the number**.

Then **Henseleit** stepped onto the 72nd hole and ripped the whole championship back open with a birdie that shoved both players into extra holes at **5-under**.

That is the kind of finish that can make a first-time major contender look rattled.

Kuwaki did not really do rattled.

The LPGA and AIG Women's Open reports both say she closed it out on the **second playoff hole**, turning a dramatic Sunday into her **first LPGA Tour title** and her **first major championship** in the same swing.

That is a hell of a way to arrive.

## This Was Not Supposed To Be a One-Woman Script Anyway

One reason this finish works so well is that it validated the broader point we made before the week even started.

This championship never needed to be framed like it belonged to one obvious star.

Yes, **Nelly Korda** came in with the **Rolex Rankings No. 1** label and the **Rolex ANNIKA Major Award** race sitting right there.
Yes, there were familiar names all over the board.

But the event kept feeling bigger than one player all week, and Sunday paid that off.

Instead of the major collapsing into a predictable coronation, it gave the tournament room for:

- **Kuwaki** to become a major winner
- **Henseleit** to deliver the shot of the championship's closing hour
- and the 50th edition to finish with actual tension instead of commemorative fluff

That is a much better ending than "biggest name wins because golf media already wrote the homepage deck on Thursday."

## Yealimi Noh Could Not Quite Hold the Lead Together

**Reuters** reported that **Yealimi Noh**, who led after three rounds, finished **third at 4-under** after a closing **74**.

That is the painful other side of major Sunday.

Overnight leads at links venues can look solid right up until the day starts asking harder questions. Royal Lytham did exactly that. It made enough people uncomfortable, and it let the tournament stay open long enough for Kuwaki and Henseleit to drag everyone into the kind of finish majors are supposed to produce.

## This Is Why the AIG Women's Open Felt Legit All Week

The best thing about the result is that it did not feel random.

It felt like the event had enough real weight to produce a winner people had to take seriously.

That matters because the whole championship has been trying to act more like a proper major lately, from purse and coverage growth to field strength and venue framing. We already wrote about that in [our earlier piece on the AIG finally behaving like a major](/blog/aig-womens-open-is-acting-like-a-major-2026/) and in [the Georgia Hall Royal Lytham return column](/blog/georgia-hall-royal-lytham-comeback-opinion-2026/).

Sunday gave that whole argument another push.

The winner was not prepackaged.
The finish was messy in the good way.
The board had enough depth to feel dangerous.

That is what a real major looks like.
