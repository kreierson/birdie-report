## Why e6 Soft Still Has a Very Real Case

The **e6 Soft** exists because plenty of golfers do not need a more expensive ball. They need a softer ball that launches easier, goes far enough, and does not punish them at checkout.

Bridgestone's own current language is blunt:

- soft feel
- long distance
- **softer and larger core** for faster compression
- **soft seamless cover** for spin and feel

That is a very normal-golfer product story, and I mean that as praise.

If you are the player who:

- swings at normal human speeds
- still loses balls
- does not care about wringing the last bit of wedge nuance out of a mid-price ball

then the **e6 Soft** makes a ton of sense.

That is also why it keeps holding up well across [Best Golf Balls for Seniors 2026](/blog/best-golf-balls-seniors-2026/), [Best Golf Balls for High Handicappers](/blog/best-golf-balls-high-handicappers/), and the softer-value comparison set already built around it.

## Feel: Both Friendly, But the Buying Logic Is Different

Both of these balls are easy enough to like.

But they are soft for different reasons.

The **e6 Soft** is soft because Bridgestone wants you to buy a softer, easier, longer cheap ball.

The **Tour Response** is soft because TaylorMade wants you to feel like you moved into a more serious performance category without jumping all the way into the flagship-tax lane.

That means:

- **e6 Soft** is soft as part of a simpler value story
- **Tour Response** is soft as part of a broader performance-value story

If all you want is a softer ball you can rebuy without blinking, the e6 wins.

If you want soft feel plus a more serious short-game case, the Tour Response wins.

## Long-Game Distance: e6 Soft Has the Cleaner Simplicity

The **e6 Soft** has the more direct long-game pitch.

Bridgestone flat-out says the larger softer core is there for **faster compression** and **longer distance**. That is the ball's job.

The **Tour Response** is not bad off the tee. It is just solving a wider problem. TaylorMade is selling more of an all-around "play better golf" ball than a pure soft-distance answer.

So if your actual question is:

"Which one is easier to recommend to a slower swinger who just wants simpler distance help?"

the answer is **e6 Soft**.

## Short-Game Value: Tour Response Wins the Reason People Pay More

This is where the extra money starts making sense.

The **Tour Response** is a value-urethane ball. That matters because it gives the ball a much more believable short-game story than the e6 Soft can really make.

That does not mean the **e6 Soft** is clumsy or unusable. It is fine for its lane.

It does mean the **Tour Response** is easier to recommend if your buying logic includes:

- more greenside credibility
- a more complete feel profile
- a better reason to pay above the cheap-soft-ball tier
