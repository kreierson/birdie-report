The 30–80 yard zone becomes difficult when every shot is a different guess.

One day the golfer shortens a full swing. The next day the grip changes, the face opens, the ball moves back, and the downswing slows down. There is no stable reference, so even solid contact produces an uncertain distance.

The fix is a matrix:

- a small number of wedges,
- three repeatable swing lengths,
- one stock tempo,
- and **carry numbers** measured with your ball.

The matrix does not make every lie identical. It gives you a baseline so you know what to adjust.

## Carry Distance Comes First

Separate two numbers:

- **Carry:** how far the ball flies before its first bounce.
- **Total:** where it finishes after bounce and roll.

Carry is the transferable number. Total distance changes with green firmness, slope, spin, wind, landing surface, and ball.

If a front bunker requires 52 yards to clear and the pin is at 60, “my wedge goes about 60” is not enough. You need a stock motion that carries at least 52 under the current conditions.

## Build the Three-Swing System

Use body checkpoints instead of trying to swing at percentages:

| Swing label | Backswing checkpoint | Through-swing goal | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short | hands near trail pocket or hip | matching compact finish | roughly 30–45 yards |
| Medium | lead arm near parallel | chest through, hands near lead hip/ribs | roughly 45–65 yards |
| Long | hands between chest and shoulder height | balanced three-quarter finish | roughly 60–80 yards |

Those yardage bands are organizational, not promises. A golfer's short sand-wedge carry may overlap another golfer's medium lob-wedge carry.

Keep tempo stable. Change the length of the motion, not the amount of panic in the downswing.

## The 30–80 Yard Matrix

Start with three wedges, not every club in the bag. Record the average carry from five to eight solid shots at each swing length.

| Your wedge | Short carry | Medium carry | Long carry | Typical flight |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Pitching or gap wedge | ___ | ___ | ___ | lower, more release |
| Sand wedge | ___ | ___ | ___ | stock |
| Lob wedge | ___ | ___ | ___ | higher, less release |

Write the numbers on a small card or keep them in your phone. Recheck them when you change wedges, balls, or technique.

### How to collect honest numbers

1. Warm up before measuring.
2. Use the ball you normally play.
3. Measure carry, not the final resting spot.
4. Remove obvious mishits, but do not keep only the perfect strikes.
5. Average the representative shots.
6. Record the strike and trajectory that produced the number.

A launch monitor helps, but a range with known targets, a laser, or a practice field with markers can work.

## The Stock Pitch Setup

Use one neutral setup for the baseline:

- stance narrower than a full swing,
- ball near center,
- pressure slightly favoring the lead foot,
- grip down for control,
- chest centered,
- and the clubface square or only slightly open.

Allow the chest to rotate through. Holding the body still and throwing the hands at the ball makes low point and speed harder to repeat.

The shaft can lean slightly forward, but excessive handle press removes bounce and makes soft turf or a steep delivery less forgiving.

## Choose the Trajectory After the Number

Start with the carry required, then choose the flight.

| Situation | Preferred baseline | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Open entrance, plenty of green | lower flight | less loft, land shorter, allow release |
| Firm green | higher flight or safer landing zone | more loft if lie supports it |
| Into wind | controlled lower flight | more club, shorter swing, stable tempo |
| Downwind | expect more release | land shorter or add loft |
| Front obstacle | carry-first shot | choose a matrix number that clears it comfortably |
| Back pin with firm release | land well short | use lower-lofted wedge and total-distance plan |

Do not add a “special” trajectory unless the shot requires it. One stock flight under pressure is more valuable than five shots you barely practice.

## Adjust for the Lie

The matrix is built from a clean, level lie. On the course:

### Tight lie

- Use a neutral face and shallow brush.
- Reduce excessive forward press.
- Favor the club and swing you can strike predictably.
- Consider a lower trajectory if there is room.

### Sitting up in rough

- Expect the club to slide under the ball.
- Grip down and stay centered.
- Plan for variable spin and distance.

### Buried rough

- Use more loft and speed.
- Expect grass to reduce clean face contact.
- Choose a conservative landing area and allow rollout.

### Wet or soft turf

- Use enough bounce.
- Keep the chest turning so the handle does not stall.
- Avoid forcing the leading edge steeply into the ground.

### Uneven lie

- Match shoulders to the slope.
- Shorten the swing.
- Accept a wider distance window.

## Three Distance-Control Drills

### 1. Carry Ladder

Set targets at 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, and 80 yards.

- Hit one ball to each target in order.
- Return down the ladder.
- Score one point for finishing inside a defined carry window.
- Repeat with the same wedge, then use two wedges.

The goal is changing distance without changing tempo.

### 2. Matrix Test

Hit one ball with each wedge/swing combination—nine shots for a three-wedge matrix.

Record:

- intended carry,
- actual carry,
- strike quality,
- and left/right start.

Retest monthly. If one cell remains unreliable, remove it from the on-course matrix until it improves.

### 3. Random Nine

Use nine random distances between 30 and 80 yards.

- One ball per distance.
- Complete pre-shot routine.
- Choose carry, club, and swing length.
- Score the absolute carry error.

Your goal is reducing total error over time, not producing one spectacular shot.

## A Simple On-Course Decision

From every pitch distance:

1. Measure the front, flag, and back.
2. Identify the required carry over trouble.
3. Choose the landing zone.
4. Select the matrix carry.
5. Adjust for lie, wind, elevation, and firmness.
6. Commit to the matching swing length.

### Example: 58 yards to a front pin over a bunker

The bunker carry is 51 and the safe section behind the flag extends to 68.

Choose the matrix shot that normally carries around 60–62 rather than trying to land exactly at 58. The slightly long miss remains on the green; the short miss is sand. The target should reflect that asymmetry.

### Example: 72 yards to a back pin on a firm green

If the green releases, a 65-yard carry with a lower-lofted wedge may be smarter than flying a high wedge to 72 and hoping it stops.

That is why carry and total need separate plans.

## Troubleshooting Pitch-Shot Misses

| Miss | Likely cause | First correction |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy contact | pressure drifts back or chest stalls | stay centered and rotate through |
| Thin contact | rising through strike or low point too far back | keep chest level and brush after ball |
| Carries short with good contact | swing checkpoint or club does not match number | use the next matrix cell |
| Carries long under pressure | backswing grows or tempo spikes | rehearse the finish and keep length defined |
| Balloons into wind | too much loft/spin | use more club and a shorter motion |
| Unpredictable rollout | planning total instead of carry | pick a landing zone and calibrate first bounce |

## The Practice Plan

### Week 1: Build

- Measure every matrix cell.
- Keep only repeatable combinations.
- Create the carry card.

### Week 2: Calibrate

- Repeat the matrix from a second lie.
- Add uphill/downhill or wind notes.
- Test the carry ladder.

### Week 3 and beyond: Randomize

- Spend most practice time on one-ball random distances.
- Retest the matrix periodically.
- Track on-course misses as short, long, left, right, or contact.

Pair this with [how to practice with purpose](/blog/how-to-practice-with-purpose/) so the wedge work has a measurable score instead of becoming another pile of raked range balls.

## The Bottom Line

Own the 30–80 yard zone by reducing variables:

- three wedges,
- three swing lengths,
- one stock tempo,
- measured carry numbers,
- and a clear adjustment for lie and trajectory.
