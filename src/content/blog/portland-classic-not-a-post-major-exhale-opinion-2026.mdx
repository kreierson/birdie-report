The **Portland Classic** is one of those LPGA weeks that gets subtly disrespected by the calendar.

The **AIG Women's Open** ends.
Everyone exhales.
Then Portland shows up and gets treated like a decent little reset before the next thing.

That framing is lazy.
And this year's field makes it look even dumber than usual.

Official **LPGA** materials checked on **Thursday, August 13, 2026** show the **54th Portland Classic** opening at **Columbia Edgewater Country Club** as the **longest-running non-major on the LPGA schedule**, with a **144-player field**, **$2 million purse**, **nine players from the Rolex top 25**, **seven 2026 LPGA winners**, and three high-end amateur champions led by **Maria Jose Marin**, **Farah O'Keefe**, and **Valentine Delon**.

That is not "cooldown week."

That is a perfectly respectable LPGA event being undersold out of habit.

This column is based on the **LPGA's August 11 field breakdown**, **August 12 featured-groups story**, and the current **how-to-watch page**, all checked on **Thursday, August 13, 2026**. No pretending I have a spiritually deeper understanding of Portland because I once looked at a scorecard over coffee.

## The Field Alone Should Kill the Throwaway-Week Talk

The LPGA's official field page says Portland has:

- **Jeeno Thitikul** at **No. 2**
- **Haeran Ryu** at **No. 3**
- **Lottie Woad** at **No. 5**
- **Ruoning Yin** at **No. 8**
- **Aki Iwai** back as defending champion at **No. 19**

It also says the field includes **seven winners from this LPGA season**, including **Ryu**, **Thitikul**, **Woad**, **Lauren Coughlin**, **Celine Boutier**, **Yana Wilson**, and **Gina Kim**.

So what exactly are we cooling down from?

If you have top-end stars, active winners, and a defending champion with real status, maybe the answer is not that the event is too small.
Maybe the answer is that golf people are too conditioned to flatten everything that lives right after a major.

## The LPGA Should Be Better at Holding the Story After a Major

This is a recurring weakness in golf coverage.

A major week ends and the sport immediately behaves like the next stop has to re-earn its existence from zero.

That is stupid.

Good leagues build continuity.
They do not act like every event not labeled "major" has to crawl back into relevance one Monday at a time.

The LPGA actually has the tools to do this better right now because the women's game has enough identifiable current threads:

- **Ryu** still feels hot every time she tees it up
- **Thitikul** is a weekly standard-setter
- **Woad** is already past the "promising" label, which we argued [here](/blog/lottie-woad-second-lpga-win-opinion-2026/)
- **Aki Iwai** is not some anonymous defending champ
- and the amateur pipeline still has juice through names like [Farah O'Keefe](/blog/farah-okeefe-ncaa-title-us-womens-open-2026/)

That is a connected season if anyone bothers to present it like one.

## Portland Also Has Its Own Identity, Which Matters

The official LPGA featured-groups piece reminds people that this is the **54th playing** of the event and the **longest-running non-major** on the schedule.

That should matter.

Not in some fake reverent sense where we all clap because a tournament has been around a while.
It matters because long-running events should feel like fixtures, not placeholders.

Portland has:

- a recognizable course in **Columbia Edgewater**
- a repeatable spot on the calendar
- enough history to feel like an actual tour stop instead of a temporary sponsor activation
- and, this week, a field strong enough to justify the heritage language

That is a healthier mix than a lot of men's events get, frankly.

## The Amateur Layer Makes the Week Better, Not Cuter

One thing I like about this field is that the amateur story is serious without being forced.

The LPGA says Portland is giving exemptions to:

- **Maria Jose Marin**, the **Augusta National Women's Amateur** champion
- **Farah O'Keefe**, the **NCAA individual champion**
- **Valentine Delon**, the **Women's Amateur Championship** winner

That is not a ceremonial youth corner.

That is exactly the kind of pipeline visibility women's golf should want more often. We already argued in [our LPGA growth piece](/blog/lpga-growth-finally-looks-structural-2026/) that the women's game looks stronger when the system connecting college golf, amateur golf, and pro golf feels more coherent. Portland helps with that.

It keeps the season from feeling like the same 20 names in a sealed tube.

## Lexi Thompson Is Not the Main Point, But She Helps the Week Feel Alive

The official LPGA featured-groups story also spotlights a Thursday-Friday group with **Lexi Thompson**, **Chanettee Wannasaen**, and **Lindy Duncan**.

And because this sport cannot go a week without human context attached to a big name, the same LPGA story notes Thompson announced she is **pregnant with her first child**, due in **February 2027**, while still making a limited run of starts this season.

That does not transform Portland into some celebrity-story week.

It does, however, reinforce the actual point:
this tournament is not lacking recognizable names or live context.

## My Take

The LPGA does not always need a new gimmick, a new format, or a new slogan.

Sometimes it just needs to stop apologizing for good events that happen at inconvenient spots on the calendar.

Portland is one of those events.

If you give me:

- **nine top-25 players**
- **seven current-season winners**
- a defending champion with real profile
- three elite amateur champions
- and a tournament with more than half a century of history

I am not calling that an exhale week.

I am calling that a tour stop the league should sell with more confidence.

## Bottom Line

Checked on **Thursday, August 13, 2026**, official **LPGA** materials show the **Portland Classic** opening with a strong field, real current-season star power, meaningful amateur depth, and the longest-running non-major slot on the schedule.

That deserves better than post-major cooldown framing.

The LPGA has enough good weeks already.
It should stop acting like only the majors are allowed to feel important.
