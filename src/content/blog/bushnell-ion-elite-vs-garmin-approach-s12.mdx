Buy the **Bushnell iON Elite** if you want the better golf value.

Buy the **Garmin Approach S12** if you want the simpler watch and the much better battery story.

That is the page.

These two watches sit close enough in price that golfers keep pretending the decision should be harder than it is. It is not.

The **iON Elite** gives you the richer golf feature set: **slope**, **HoleView**, **Shot Planning**, **GreenView**, **Dynamic Green Mapping**, and a color touchscreen for roughly **$219.99** on Bushnell Golf's current product page. The **S12** gives you Garmin's button-based anti-annoyance lane: **42,000-plus** courses, **Big Numbers mode**, hazard yardages, and **up to 30 hours** of GPS battery life for about **$199.99**.

This is a **research-based comparison** built from Bushnell Golf's current **iON Elite** product page, Garmin's current **Approach S12** product materials and support docs, and Birdie Report's existing GPS-watch cluster checked on **August 7, 2026**. No fake "I alternated them for ten twilight rounds and found my true self" routine.

## Quick Verdict

For most golfers shopping around two hundred bucks, I would recommend the **Bushnell iON Elite** first.

I would only lean **Garmin Approach S12** first if I knew the buyer wanted:

- absolute simplicity
- buttons over touch controls
- the best battery life in the matchup
- the fewest possible ways for the watch to become annoying

That is the real split.

## Why the Bushnell iON Elite Wins for Most Golfers

At basically same-budget money, the **iON Elite** just gives you more golf stuff that people actually use.

Bushnell's current page still highlights:

- slope-compensated distances
- HoleView
- Shot Planning
- GreenView with movable pin placement
- Dynamic Green Mapping
- **12+ hours** of battery

That is a very solid list at this price.

More importantly, it is a very solid list for the golfer who plays unfamiliar courses, wants more help choosing layup lines, or just wants a little more context than front-middle-back and vibes.

That is why the **iON Elite** keeps holding up in the cluster, especially in the full [Bushnell iON Elite review](/blog/bushnell-ion-elite-gps-watch-review/), [Bushnell iON Elite vs Shot Scope V5](/blog/bushnell-ion-elite-vs-shot-scope-v5/), and the broader [Best Golf GPS Watches for Seniors 2026](/blog/best-golf-gps-watches-seniors-2026/) page.

## Why the Garmin Approach S12 Still Has a Legit Case

The **S12** stays relevant because it refuses to get cute.

It gives you:

- front, middle, and back yardages
- hazard and dogleg numbers
- button controls
- Big Numbers mode
- **up to 30 hours** of GPS battery

That is the whole value proposition, and it is a good one.

The S12 is better if you are the kind of golfer who reads a Bushnell feature list and immediately thinks:

"That all sounds useful, but I know I am going to want the simplest thing possible by hole six."

That golfer should not talk themselves into the Bushnell just because the feature sheet looks better.

The **S12** is the better answer when your top priority is a watch that stays in its lane.

## Same Money, Different Kind of Value

The **iON Elite** is better value if your definition of value is:

- more golf features
- more hole context
- more help on unfamiliar layouts

The **S12** is better value if your definition of value is:

- longer battery life
- faster learning curve
- fewer menus
- less friction over time

That is why both watches can be smart buys for different golfers without the page becoming wishy-washy.

I just think more golfers will benefit from the **iON Elite** side of that split.
