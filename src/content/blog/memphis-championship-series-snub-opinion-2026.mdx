If the **FedEx St. Jude Championship** is not top-tier enough for the **PGA TOUR's** future premium map, then the Tour is not just trimming a city.

It is exposing its priorities.

According to **Associated Press** reporting published on **Wednesday, August 6, 2026** and **Golf Channel** reporting published the same day, both checked again on **Sunday, August 9**, the **FedEx St. Jude Championship** will **not** be part of the top-tier **Championship Series** when the Tour's new two-series model begins in **2028**. Golf Channel's report adds that the Tour's reshaped postseason is likely to become a **two-event** model, while AP reported Memphis is still under consideration for a **Challenger Series** slot rather than the premium layer.

That is a bigger tell than the Tour probably wants it to be.

This column is based on AP's August 6, 2026 report, Golf Channel's August 6, 2026 reporting, and the PGA TOUR's own previously announced 2028 structure that Birdie Report already covered in June and July. No pretending I got slipped the city-ranking spreadsheet and a future sponsor deck over airport coffee.

For the underlying structure, read [our June explainer on Brian Rolapp's two-series model](/blog/pga-tour-championship-series-challenger-series-2028-rolapp/), [our opinion on why no sponsor exemptions was one of the few obviously healthy ideas in the plan](/blog/pga-tour-no-sponsor-exemptions-right-call-opinion-2026/), and [our Sompo/Napa story on how the Tour has already started attaching real markets to the 2028 map](/blog/sompo-pga-tour-silverado-championship-series-2026/).

## The Awkward Part Is Not Memphis. It Is the FedExCup Branding.

Memphis missing the upper tier would already be significant on its own.

But Memphis is not just some random city on the current map.
It is the site of the **FedEx St. Jude Championship**, the first playoff event, in the city most tied to **FedEx** itself.

So when AP reports that this event will not be part of the top tier in **2028**, the obvious follow-up is not just "what happens to Memphis?"

It is:
what exactly is the **FedExCup** supposed to be if its own front-door event is no longer premium inventory?

That question gets harder, not easier, when you remember the Tour keeps talking about sharper structure, cleaner premium weeks, and easier-to-follow stakes.

If the playoff opener in the FedEx city is not premium enough, then either the playoffs are being redefined so aggressively that the old branding center no longer matters, or the Tour has decided market logic beats legacy even when the legacy is carrying the sponsor name.

Neither answer is small.

## The Tour Is Choosing Its Future Cities Like a League, Not a Circuit

This has been the direction for months.

The June 23 structure announcement already told us the Tour wants a more league-like product, with **Championship Series** stops functioning as the cleanest premium inventory. The later **Sompo** announcement told us the map is also going to be about sponsor fit, market fit, and which locations can serve the new architecture cleanly.

Memphis getting nudged out confirms the colder version of that logic.

Tradition helps until it collides with the new portfolio.
Then the new portfolio wins.

That does not make the decision automatically wrong.
It does make the decision revealing.

## This Is Why the "Regular Season Matters More" Pitch Needs to Be Better

Golf Channel reported that the new model will shift more focus to the **regular-season winner** and allow the Tour to rethink the postseason.

Fine.
There is a coherent argument in there.

But if the Tour wants the public to buy into that pitch, it has to say something more convincing than "trust us, we are reimagining things."

Because from the outside, this can also look like the Tour deciding that the easiest way to sell a premium product is to keep moving the pins toward whatever market package feels freshest.

And that is exactly where fans start getting suspicious.

You cannot spend years telling people the **FedExCup Playoffs** are the season's closing spine, then casually strip the playoff opener of future top-tier status and act like nobody is supposed to notice the contradiction.

## FedEx Being Disappointed Is the Least Surprising Part

Golf Channel cited a **FedEx** statement expressing disappointment, and AP reported a similar reaction while noting the company still wants to stay involved in Memphis and with **St. Jude**.

Of course it does.

FedEx has spent too much time and too much brand equity on this part of the schedule for the shrug version of the story to make any sense.

The more interesting thing is not that FedEx is unhappy.
The interesting thing is that the Tour seems willing to tolerate that unhappiness in service of the new map.

That tells you how serious the Tour is about rearranging the whole product around the **2028** structure.

## My Take

I do not think this automatically means the future model is bad.

I do think it means the Tour should stop pretending this is just a harmless modernization exercise.

It is a values decision.

When you tell Memphis that the **FedEx St. Jude Championship** is no longer premium-tier material, you are saying:

- city history matters less than the new inventory plan
- playoff branding matters less than the new inventory plan
- sponsor symbolism matters less than the new inventory plan

Again, maybe that is the right call.

But then own it.
Do not dress it up like everybody should simply admire the efficiency.

Because the whole reason this lands hard is that Memphis has been one of the clearest places where the FedExCup actually felt attached to something tangible.

Take that away, and the playoffs start looking less like a naturally rooted finish and more like a premium package still deciding what it wants to be.

## The Timing Makes the Whole Thing Sharper

This also dropped during **Wyndham week**, which is unintentionally perfect.

Right now the Tour is asking players to sweat over the top 70 line just to get into **Memphis**. We have already covered that pressure in [our Wyndham column](/blog/wyndham-championship-cruel-week-opinion-2026/), [our Saturday Hossler update](/blog/beau-hossler-wyndham-weekend-lead-2026/), and [our Sunday final-round setup piece](/blog/beau-hossler-michael-brennan-wyndham-sunday-2026/).

So the current product is still telling players and fans:
get to Memphis, because Memphis matters.

At the same time, the future product is quietly saying:
Memphis might not matter the way you thought.

That contradiction is the story.

## Bottom Line

Checked on **Sunday, August 9, 2026**, current **AP** and **Golf Channel** reporting still says the **FedEx St. Jude Championship** is set to miss the PGA TOUR's top-tier **Championship Series** in **2028**, even as Memphis remains in the conversation for some other future role.

That is not just a local-market update.

It is one of the clearest signs yet that the Tour's new model is going to prioritize premium-event architecture over legacy, symbolism, and even one of the most obvious homes the **FedExCup** has.
